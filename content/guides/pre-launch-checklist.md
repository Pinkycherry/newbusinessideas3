# The pre-launch checklist for a business with no budget

Most pre-launch checklists are procrastination with a tick box next to it. This one is deliberately short, and split into what actually blocks a first sale and what does not.

## Before your first customer pays

**A one-sentence answer to "what do you do".** Who you help and what changes for them. If you need a paragraph, the buyer will not repeat it to anyone.

**A price you will say out loud without flinching.** Decide it before the conversation, not during. A price invented under pressure is always too low.

**A way to be contacted and a way to be paid.** A phone number that gets answered and a payment method that works. Nothing more sophisticated than that is required to earn money.

**Knowing what one sale leaves you.** Price minus what that sale costs you to deliver. That is your [contribution margin](/founder-glossary/contribution-margin), and if it is thin or negative the business is broken before it starts. The [break-even calculator](/calculator/break-even) turns it into the number of sales you actually need.

**One named person to ask first.** Not a launch. One person, by name, this week.

That is the whole blocking list. Everything after this can happen while you are already earning.

## Worth sorting early, but not first

**A separate account for business money.** A [current account](/founder-glossary/current-account) keeps business money out of your personal spending, which is the difference between knowing what you earn and guessing.

**Registration, at the level your business actually needs.** For most people starting alone in India, a [sole proprietorship](/founder-glossary/sole-proprietorship) is the starting point, and [Udyam registration](/founder-glossary/udyam-registration) is free on the government portal. [GST](/founder-glossary/goods-and-services-tax) becomes compulsory above a turnover threshold and for certain kinds of business — check the current rules on gst.gov.in rather than taking a figure from an article, including this one.

**A simple invoice.** What was supplied, the amount, the due date. Raising one is not being paid, and the gap between the two is where small businesses run out of cash.

**Somewhere the work is visible.** Two or three examples of the actual thing. Not a brand, not a logo — proof.

## What to skip

A logo. A company name you have agonised over. A website before you have a customer. Business cards. A brand palette. An office. A laptop, in many cases. Any tool on a paid plan when a free one does the job.

Each of these feels like progress and produces no evidence that anyone will pay. They are the things founders do instead of the one uncomfortable thing on the first list.

## The order that works

Pick an idea you can deliver by hand this week. Say what it is in one sentence and decide the price. Ask one named person. Deliver it badly but completely. Then use what you learned — and the money — to sort out the second list.
