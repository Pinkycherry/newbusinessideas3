# Product-market fit, and how to tell you do not have it yet

Product-market fit is the point where enough people want what you have made that selling stops being a fight. Before it, every sale is pushed. After it, some of them pull.

That is the whole definition. It is a feeling before it is a metric, and the feeling is specific: demand arrives slightly faster than you can handle it, and you start worrying about delivery instead of about finding the next customer.

## The four signals that mean something

**People come back without being chased.** [Retention](/founder-glossary/retention) is the single most honest signal, because it cannot be bought. Someone paying you a second time has tested the thing and chosen it again.

**Customers explain it to each other.** When a buyer describes what you do to someone else in their own words — and gets it right — the offer is clear enough to spread without you in the room.

**Sales get shorter, not longer.** Less explaining, fewer objections, faster yes. If every conversation still takes the full hour of persuasion it took a year ago, nothing has clicked yet.

**Losing a customer would hurt them, not just you.** Ask what they would do if you stopped tomorrow. "Find someone else by Friday" is a service. "I genuinely don't know" is fit.

## The three that only look like it

**Traffic and followers.** Attention is not demand. An audience that will not pay is a cost, not an asset.

**Compliments.** People are kind, especially to someone trying. Praise from a non-buyer is worth exactly nothing as evidence.

**One big customer.** A single client covering your costs feels like fit and is actually concentration risk. It tells you one company has this problem. That is a start, not a pattern.

## What to do while you do not have it

Most founders spend this period doing the wrong thing: spending more on being seen. Advertising a product nobody returns to just buys a larger audience for the same disappointment, faster.

Three things are worth doing instead.

**Talk to the people who left.** They know exactly what is wrong, and they are the only group with no reason to be polite about it. This is the cheapest research available and almost nobody does it.

**Narrow, don't broaden.** When something is not working the instinct is to add features and widen the audience. The move that usually works is the opposite: pick the narrowest group who liked it most and serve only them, completely. See [niche](/founder-glossary/niche) and [beachhead market](/founder-glossary/beachhead-market).

**Keep the burn low enough to stay in the game.** Fit is found by iteration, and iteration takes months. Months cost money. Run your numbers through the [runway calculator](/calculator/runway) and treat the answer as the real deadline — not the launch date you wrote down.

## The thing nobody says

Plenty of good businesses never reach product-market fit in the way the phrase implies, and do perfectly well anyway. A local service with steady repeat customers, a freelancer with a full book, a shop people walk past and into — none of those pull in the way a product does, and all of them pay.

Fit is a useful idea for a business that wants to grow faster than a founder's own hours. It is an unhelpful yardstick for a business meant to replace a salary. Deciding honestly which one you are building saves you from chasing a signal that was never relevant to you.

## Where to start

If you have not sold anything yet, this guide is not your next step — [validation](/startup-guides/business-idea-validation) is. Fit is something you look for after the first customers, not before them.

If you already have a handful of customers and cannot tell whether anything has clicked, start with retention. Count how many bought twice. Then look at the models in [online business ideas](/category/online-business-ideas) and [part-time business ideas](/category/part-time-business-ideas), where a repeat purchase is built into the offer rather than hoped for — [Part Time Bookkeeping For One Retained Client](/idea/part-time-bookkeeping-one-client) is the clearest example of that shape.
