# Free CRM for small business owners in India, and the four CRM setups we did in Kashi for a jeweller, an incense maker, a coach and a manufacturer

## The notebook that every shop keeps

Every small business I have ever visited keeps a customer notebook, even the ones that say they do not. Subramanya Sharma keeps his in a red cloth-bound register under the jewellery counter. Dinesh Gupta keeps his in a diary with incense stains on every page. Priyanka Rathod keeps hers in three different places, a spiral pad, a phone contact list and her memory. Sudhanshu keeps his in his head, which, he admits, is the worst place of all.

[IMAGE 1]

When Shankar Kaka and I came back to Varanasi after the trip to Patna and Gaya, we sat on the steps near Harishchandra Ghat one evening and counted. In this journey alone we had met more than a hundred small business owners, and not one of them had a proper system for remembering their customers. They had relationships, which matter more, but the relationships lived in notebooks that could be lost, soaked or forgotten.

So we made a decision that evening, with the river moving quietly in front of us. For the next few days, before anything else, we would set up a customer relationship system for four people we had come to know well, using only free or nearly free tools, and we would write down exactly what worked. This article is that record, written in the mornings after each setup.

## Why a free CRM for small business is the first tool, not the last

A CRM, a customer relationship management system, sounds like something for large companies with sales teams. In truth, it is just a better notebook. It remembers who your customers are, what they bought, what they asked for, when you last spoke and what you promised to do next.

For a small business, the value of a free CRM for small business use is not technology. It is memory. The jeweller who remembers that a family is planning a wedding in the coming season, the coach who remembers which student asked about a weekend batch, the manufacturer who remembers which buyer needs a quotation every quarter, all of them sell more without spending a rupee on advertising.

Kaka, who has guided pilgrims through Kashi for many years, understood this immediately. "I remember every family I have guided," he said. "That is why they send their relatives to me. A CRM is just a memory that does not get old."

I have written before that [word of mouth beats advertising for a young business](https://ethicalfounder.in/word-of-mouth-vs-ads-for-a-first-year-business/), and a CRM is what turns word of mouth into something you can rely on. It reminds you to call back, to follow up and to thank the person who referred you.

## The three tools we chose: Zoho CRM free edition, Bigin by Zoho and Twenty CRM

Before the first setup, I sat with Kaka and wrote down what each person actually needed. Most small businesses in India do not need a complex system. They need something free to start, simple enough to use on a phone, in their own language of work, and able to grow when they grow.

We settled on three options, each for a different kind of business. The first was the [Zoho CRM free edition](https://www.zoho.com/crm/lp/zoho-crm-free.html), which Zoho offers free for up to three users and which includes lead and contact management, tasks, reports and basic workflows. Zoho is an Indian company, and that matters to many owners, both for trust and for support in Indian time zones, a point I have touched on in [my piece on India's indigenous technology future](https://ethicalfounder.in/indias-indigenous-ai-development-will-lead-the-future/).

The second was Bigin by Zoho, a simpler pipeline-style CRM built for very small teams, which has a free plan for a single user and low-cost paid plans priced per user per month in rupees. Bigin by Zoho is the one I recommend most often to shop owners, because it shows your customers as cards moving through stages, which is how most traders already think.

The third was something newer, and I want to spend time on it because I believe it shows where this category is going. [Twenty CRM](https://twenty.com/pricing) is an open source CRM that describes itself as an agentic CRM, meaning it is designed so that AI agents can handle routine CRM chores such as logging meetings, enriching contact details and updating records. It can be self-hosted for free by anyone with the technical help to run it, or used as a paid cloud service. For a small Indian business with a tech-savvy family member or partner, open source CRM India adoption like this could change the cost equation completely.

## Setup one: a CRM setup for shops at Subramanya's jewellery counter

We started with Subramanya Sharma, because his need was the clearest. His imitation jewellery shop lives on wedding seasons, repeat families and a handful of trusted resellers in other parts of the city. His red register had years of names in it, but no way to know which families were planning a wedding next, which resellers had paid and which had not, and which customers had not visited in a year.

[IMAGE 2]

We chose Bigin by Zoho for him, because he is the only user and because the pipeline view fits his trade. We created three pipelines. The first was "wedding families," with stages for enquiry, visit, selection, order and delivered. The second was "resellers," with stages for sample sent, first order, regular and paused. The third was "repairs and returns," which he said he had never tracked at all.

The CRM setup for shops like his took one long afternoon. We entered his top sixty families from the register by hand, one card at a time, with the wedding month noted in a custom field, and we added his five resellers with their payment habits. By evening, for the first time, he could see on his phone which families were expected to shop in the coming wedding season.

Subramanya scrolled through the pipeline for a long time without speaking. Then he said, "My father kept all of this in his head. He never forgot a family. I have been forgetting them, and I did not know it." Kaka put a hand on his shoulder and said that even Bholenath keeps a record of every soul, and nobody calls that disrespect.

## Setup two: Dinesh's incense workshop and the temple shop buyers

Dinesh Gupta's agarbatti workshop on the edge of the city had a different problem. His buyers are temple-road shops and a few wholesalers, and his trouble was not memory of families but memory of orders: who orders which fragrance, how often, and who is due for a visit.

[IMAGE 3]

For him we chose the Zoho CRM free edition, because his son and his workshop supervisor also needed access, and the free plan covers three users. We set up accounts for each buying shop, contacts for the person who places orders, and a simple deals stage for each repeat order cycle. We added one custom field Dinesh insisted on: the shop's preferred fragrance, because, he said, "If I bring sandalwood to a shop that sells only rose, I have wasted the morning."

The free edition's tasks and reminders did the rest. Each shop now has a reminder to visit on its usual reorder cycle, and the supervisor can log a visit from his phone while standing at the shop counter. I deliberately kept this setup small, because a CRM that is too complicated on the first day is abandoned on the second.

Dinesh's wife, who handles the women artisans' wages, asked whether the CRM could also hold the artisans' details. I said it could, but I advised against mixing customers and staff in one system. For a small unit, a separate simple register for staff is cleaner, and I would rather Dinesh master one tool before adding another.

## Setup three: a sales pipeline for small business coaching in Priyanka Rathod's office

Priyanka Rathod, the business coach we met in Patna, joined us in Varanasi for two days, and her setup was the most interesting. Her business runs on enquiries: people who hear about her classes, call, attend one free session, and then either enrol or disappear. She had no idea how many enquiries she received each month or why some people did not enrol.

[IMAGE 4]

We built her a sales pipeline for small business coaching in Bigin by Zoho, with stages for enquiry, free session attended, follow-up call, enrolled and alumni. We added a field for how each person heard about her, which immediately revealed something: almost all her enrolled students had come through referrals from earlier students, not from her paid posters.

That single insight changed her plans. She decided to stop spending on posters and to start a small referral thank-you for past students, a simple certificate and a free advanced session. Priyanka said the pipeline had shown her in two days what she had been guessing at for three years.

She also asked about the newer agentic CRM tools she had been reading about, the kind that summarise calls and update records on their own. I showed her how Twenty CRM approaches this, with agents that can log activity and enrich contacts, and we discussed when it would make sense for her. My advice was to start with the simpler pipeline now and consider an agentic CRM once her team grows beyond herself and one assistant, because AI that saves time is valuable only when there is enough data and volume to save time on.

## Setup four: a CRM for manufacturers and customer data ownership at Sudhanshu's factory

Sudhanshu's fastener unit in Mumbai was the setup we did remotely, on a long video call from Kaka's house, with Sahadev joining from the village. A CRM for manufacturers looks different from a shop's CRM, because the customers are businesses, orders repeat on quotations, and the most important information is technical: sizes, grades, finishes and delivery schedules.

[IMAGE 5]

We used the Zoho CRM free edition again for him, structured around accounts for each buying company, contacts for purchase managers, and deals for each quotation. We added custom fields for the product grade, the usual monthly quantity and the payment terms each buyer has agreed. Sahadev, the strategist, added a field I liked: "reason we lost this quotation," so that Sudhanshu can learn from every order he does not win.

I also raised a question that every manufacturer should ask before putting customer data into any cloud system: where is my data stored, and who can access it? I have written about [why SaaS data storage location matters](https://ethicalfounder.in/saas-data-storage-location-in-2026/), and for a manufacturer whose customer list is his most valuable asset, this is not a small detail. Customer data ownership, the right to export everything and leave at any time, should be checked before the first record is entered.

This is where open source options like Twenty CRM become interesting for manufacturers. A self-hosted CRM keeps the data on infrastructure the business controls, which some owners prefer strongly. The trade-off is that someone has to maintain it. For Sudhanshu, we agreed to start on Zoho and revisit the question once his records are clean and his team is used to the habit.

## Open source CRM India teams can run: what self-hosting really takes

Sahadev, the IIT-trained strategist, pushed me hardest on the open source question, and his questions are worth sharing because many readers will have the same ones. Open source CRM India businesses can use means the software's code is public and can be installed on a server you control, at no licence cost. Twenty CRM is one such option, and there are others in the same family.

The licence may be free, but the work is not. Someone has to set up the server, keep the software updated, take backups, secure the login and fix problems when they arise. For a jeweller or a tea stall, that someone does not exist, which is why I recommended hosted tools for them. For a manufacturer with a nephew who studies computer science, or a coaching institute with a part-time technical person, self-hosting can be realistic.

Sahadev asked what the real benefit is, if the hosted free plans already cost nothing. I gave him three reasons. First, full control of data, which matters for businesses whose customer list is their main asset. Second, freedom to shape the system exactly to the business, since the data model can be changed. Third, no sudden price changes when the team grows, because there is no per-user licence on a self-hosted install.

He then asked the question every strategist asks: what is the risk? The main risk, I said, is that a self-hosted system fails quietly when nobody is looking after it. A backup that was never tested, a password that was never changed, an update that was never applied. Before choosing self-hosting, a business should name the person responsible and write down a simple monthly checklist, the same discipline I describe in [practical tips for SaaS founders](https://ethicalfounder.in/top-tricks-saas-founders-automation-tips/).

There is also a middle path that I expect many Indian small businesses to take in the coming years: start on a hosted free CRM, keep the data clean, and move to an agentic or open source CRM when the business has outgrown the free plan and has the people to support it. The habits you build on the simple tool carry over. A business that has learnt to log every customer conversation will get far more from AI agents later than one that starts with an advanced tool and no habits.

Kaka listened to all of this and summarised it better than I could. "First learn to walk on the ghat steps," he said. "Then you can run." I have since used that line with three other business owners. For readers curious about running AI on their own machines as well, my notes on [setting up a local AI assistant](https://ethicalfounder.in/local-ai-assistant/) explain a similar trade-off between control and convenience.

## What an agentic CRM means for a small Indian business

The word agentic is everywhere in technology news right now, and it can sound like hype. In simple terms, an agentic CRM is one where AI agents can do some of the routine work on your behalf: logging that a meeting happened, summarising what was discussed, filling in a company's details from public information, or reminding you about a customer who has gone quiet.

I have tracked how crowded the AI agent space has become in my notes on [AI agents and market saturation](https://ethicalfounder.in/ai-agents-market-saturation-2026/), and I am cautious about tools that promise to run your business for you. But a CRM is one of the few places where agents genuinely help, because CRM work is repetitive, structured and easy to check. If an agent logs a call wrongly, you can see it and fix it in seconds.

India's small businesses are adopting AI faster than many people expected, as I noted when looking at [India's generative AI adoption survey](https://ethicalfounder.in/india-gen-ai-adoption-score-survey/). The ones who benefit most are those who first build clean, simple habits, because an agent can only work with the data you give it. That is why I advise every owner to start with a simple free CRM for small business use, build the habit, and then let agents help once the foundation is solid.

## Five mistakes we saw, and how we avoided them

Across the four setups, the same mistakes tried to creep in, and naming them may save you weeks. The first is entering everything on day one. Start with your top customers only, the ones who matter most, and add the rest gradually.

The second is creating too many fields. Every extra field is a question you must answer for every customer, and owners stop filling them in. Keep only the fields you will actually use to decide something.

The third is mixing personal and business contacts. A CRM should hold business relationships, not your cousin's phone number. The fourth is not deciding who updates it. In Dinesh's workshop, we named the supervisor as the person responsible for logging every visit, and that single decision is what keeps the system alive.

There is a sixth mistake that only appeared in Priyanka's setup, and it is worth naming because coaches, tutors and consultants make it often. She wanted to record every conversation in long paragraphs. A CRM note should be short enough to read in ten seconds: what was asked, what you promised, when you will follow up. Long notes feel thorough, but nobody reads them, and the next action gets lost inside them.

The fifth is forgetting to look at it. A CRM that nobody opens is just a more expensive notebook. We asked each owner to open their CRM every morning for five minutes with their tea, the same way I recommend reviewing [daily business numbers](https://ethicalfounder.in/daily-business-reporting-automation/), and to act on one reminder before opening the shop.

## Questions People Ask About Free CRM for Small Business

**What is the best free CRM for small business owners in India?**

For a one-person shop, Bigin by Zoho's free single-user plan is a simple start, and for a small team of up to three people, the Zoho CRM free edition offers more features at no cost.

**What does the Zoho CRM free edition include?**

Zoho describes its CRM free edition as free for up to three users, with lead and contact management, tasks, reports, basic workflows and other core features, and the current list is on Zoho's official page.

**Is Bigin by Zoho free?**

Bigin has a free plan for a single user and low-cost paid plans priced per user per month for small teams, so a one-person business can start without paying.

**What is Twenty CRM?**

Twenty is an open source CRM that describes itself as an agentic CRM, with AI agents that can log activity and enrich records, and it can be self-hosted or used as a paid cloud service.

**What is an agentic CRM?**

An agentic CRM uses AI agents to handle routine CRM work such as logging meetings, summarising conversations and updating customer details, while the owner reviews and decides.

**How do I do a CRM setup for shops?**

Start with your top twenty customers, create three or four stages that match how you sell, add only the fields you will use, name one person to update it, and open it every morning.

**What should a CRM for manufacturers include?**

It should hold buying companies as accounts, purchase managers as contacts, quotations as deals, and fields for product grade, usual quantity, payment terms and reasons quotations were lost.

**Why does customer data ownership matter in a CRM?**

Your customer list is one of your most valuable assets, so check where the data is stored, who can access it and whether you can export everything before you start using any CRM.

**Can a small business build a sales pipeline for free?**

Yes, both Bigin by Zoho and the Zoho CRM free edition let you build a basic sales pipeline for small business use at no cost, with stages that match how you sell.

**Is open source CRM in India practical for small businesses?**

It can be, especially for tech-friendly teams who want control over their data, but someone must maintain a self-hosted system, so many owners start on a hosted free CRM first.
