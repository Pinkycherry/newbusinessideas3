Keep your shop's daily cash and digital collections separate from any personal investment, and review the digital side from your own records.

## A trading bot scam has a shape, five stages

At a long halt in the early morning, while the platform vendors were calling out for poha and the coach smelled of filter coffee and cardamom, I wrote out the five stages on a napkin and showed them to both men.
The soldier read it twice and nodded at every line.
The first stage is the soft entry.
A relative, a former colleague or a community leader shows you his own dashboard and his own withdrawals, and asks for nothing.
The second stage is the small test.
You put in a small amount, perhaps ₹10,000 or ₹25,000, and within days you see bot trading app returns that look modest and believable, and you withdraw a portion successfully.
This step is what makes the trading bot scam so durable, because the investor now has personal proof.
The third stage is the step-up.
The platform introduces tiers, where higher deposits unlock higher daily percentages or "premium bots", and a referral bonus appears for bringing others.
The fourth stage is the delay.
Withdrawals begin to take longer, there is talk of "server upgrades", "regulatory verification", "tax clearance" or a new app version, and investors are asked to wait or to pay a fee to release their funds.
The fifth stage is the silence.
The app stops loading, the groups are deleted, the local agent stops picking up, and the people who recruited others are left to face their own relatives.
I asked the soldier at which stage his friends had realised.
"Stage four," he said. "But they paid one fee in stage four. Everyone pays one fee."
Kaka said something about that fee which made the teacher laugh and then go quiet.
"In Kashi," he said, "the real darshan never asks you to pay first to receive what is already yours. When someone stands at a door and says pay now and you will be allowed in, look at the door, not the god."
I wrote that line on the napkin under the fifth stage.
That is the most useful thing I learnt on this first night.
The fee to release your money is the clearest trading bot scam signal there is, and almost everybody pays it once.

## The questions I ask before any automated trading platform in India

I am not against automation.
Much of my own work involves [easy business automation for Indian MSMEs](https://ethicalfounder.in/easy-business-automation-for-indian-msme/), and I believe a well-built tool can save a small business owner many hours every week.
But an automated trading platform india offer is a different thing from an automated invoice reminder.
One handles your process.
The other handles your capital and claims to beat a market that professionals with large teams struggle to beat consistently.
So before any money moves, I ask these questions.
Who is the regulated entity holding my money?
A real broker in India is registered with SEBI, and you can search the name on [SEBI's list of registered intermediaries](https://www.sebi.gov.in/intermediaries.html) in a few minutes.
If the platform cannot give you a registration number that matches a name on that list, the conversation is over.
Where is my money sitting when the bot is "trading"?
With a real broker, your funds and securities sit in your own trading and demat accounts in your own name.
With a scheme, your money sits in the promoter's account, or in a chain of accounts belonging to other people.
Can I see the trades in a contract note from a stock exchange?
A real trade leaves a record with an exchange and a contract note that you receive.
A dashboard that only exists inside the platform's app is only a picture of a record.
What is the return, and who guarantees it?
In a real market, nobody guarantees a fixed daily or monthly return.
When a platform does, you are looking at a trading bot scam, and the guarantee itself is the warning.
Why does the platform need me to bring other people?
A trading engine that earns from the market has no need for your relatives.
A trading bot scam that pays old investors with new money needs them very much.
An automated trading platform india pitch that fails even one of these questions does not deserve a second meeting.
I also keep a broader view in mind: the wider market for "AI-powered" products is crowded and overheated, as I noted in my [update on the AI market bubble](https://ethicalfounder.in/ai-market-bubble-2026-update-started/), and in a crowded market the loudest claims usually come from the weakest products.
The mine crypto meta trade 5 style of naming is one symptom of that crowding, where every new scheme reaches for the most technical-sounding words it can find.
The soldier asked me which of these he should send to his group first.
I told him the second one, about where the money sits.
It is the question most likely to make someone pause, because the answer is usually uncomfortable.

## What the bot pitch says versus what a registered intermediary does

| What the bot pitch says | What a registered broker actually does |
|---|---|
| "The bot trades for you, you only watch." | You place or authorise trades in your own account, and every trade is reported to you. |
| "Fixed daily percentage, guaranteed." | No guaranteed return; prices move both ways and you bear the risk. |
| "Send money to this UPI ID or account to activate." | Money goes only to your own trading account in your name, through the broker's verified bank. |
| "Bring five people and unlock the premium bot." | No referral targets are linked to your returns. |
| "Pay a release fee or tax first, then withdraw." | Withdrawals go to your registered bank account; taxes are handled through proper statements, never as a fee to unlock funds. |
| "Dashboard shows your profits." | Contract notes and exchange records show every trade and charge. |

## Filing a trading bot refund complaint when your money is already inside

The teacher's question was the hardest one of the night.
"What should people do who are already inside?" he asked. "They can't go back in time."
No one can, and I will not pretend that there is a clean way out.
But there are steps that improve the odds, and they should happen in a particular order.
First, stop adding money, including any "fee" to release funds.
The fee is never the last fee.
Second, take screenshots of everything: the app dashboard, the deposit receipts, the UPI transaction IDs, the chat messages with the agent, the group announcements.
Take them now, while the app still opens.
Third, write down a clear timeline: who introduced you, when you joined, how much you paid and to which account, and when withdrawals stopped.
Fourth, file a trading bot refund complaint with the police, and if a state task force or economic offences wing is already handling a case against the same platform, ask to be added to that case instead of starting alone.
Groups that file together are heard more quickly than individuals, and the evidence from many investors strengthens the case against the same accounts.
Fifth, be honest with the people you brought in.
This is the part nobody wants to hear.
The soldier's havaldar friend had brought in two relatives, and the shame of that conversation hurt him more than the money.
Speaking first, before they find out from someone else, is the only way that relationship survives.
The soldier said his friend had done exactly that, gone to each relative's house in person, touched their feet and told them the truth.
"Two of them shouted," he said. "One of them cooked him dinner."
In my understanding of karma, that walk to each house was the beginning of his repair, more than any complaint or court case could be.
For business owners, there is one more step.
Look at your books and take out the money that went into the platform from your working capital, so your accounts show what you really have.
Pretending the dashboard value still exists will push you into wrong decisions on stock, credit and staff.
If you keep a [daily business report](https://ethicalfounder.in/daily-business-reporting-automation/), this is the time to add a line for "funds outside the business" and keep it honest.

## Questions People Ask About Trading Bot Scam

**What is a trading bot scam?**

A trading bot scam is an investment scheme that claims software or a "bot" trades the market for you and pays fixed daily or monthly returns, while in reality it uses new investors' money to pay earlier investors until it collapses.

**How was the BotBro investment fraud sold to investors?**

According to Hindi reports and local posts, it was promoted as an automated platform with a dashboard showing daily gains, small early withdrawals, tiers for larger deposits and referral bonuses, before withdrawals stopped.

**Which names appear on the fake trading company list being shared in Uttarakhand?**

The list shared in local groups includes BotBro, BotAlpha, Cross Market, Mine Crypto, Meta Trade 5, Financial Freedom and TLC Coin, and each name should be checked against official records before drawing conclusions.

**Are bot trading app returns ever guaranteed?**

No registered broker or genuine market product in India can guarantee a fixed daily or monthly return, so a guarantee is itself a warning sign.

**How do I check an automated trading platform in India?**

Ask for its SEBI registration, search that name on SEBI's intermediary list, confirm your money goes only to an account in your own name, and insist on exchange contract notes for every trade.

**Why are pensioners targeted in investment fraud?**

Pensioners often hold a lump sum, have time to follow a dashboard, worry about low bank interest and trust official-looking setups, which makes them a common target for pensioners investment fraud.

**What does the Uttarakhand STF trading fraud action mean for investors?**

It creates a formal case that affected investors can attach their complaints and evidence to, which helps recovery efforts, although it does not return money on its own.

**How do I file a trading bot refund complaint?**

Stop paying any release fee, save all screenshots and transaction IDs, write a timeline, and file with the police or join an existing case with the task force or economic offences wing handling that platform.

**Is every online trading bot in India illegal?**

Algorithmic trading through a registered broker under exchange and SEBI rules is legal, but a platform that pools your money, guarantees returns and pays referral bonuses is a different thing altogether.

**What should a small business owner do if business funds went into a bot?**

Record the amount as funds outside the business, remove it from your working capital plans, file the complaint, and rebuild a cash buffer before taking on fresh stock or credit.
