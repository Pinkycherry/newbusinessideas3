In Kashi, he said later, when a stranger invites you home on the first meeting, it is not a coincidence, it is an arrangement made somewhere above.

## The brother from Mumbai and the shares that moved

Sahadev's village sits among mustard and wheat fields, with a small temple to Mahadev at the edge of the family's land and a tube well that his grandfather dug.

His elder brother, Sudhanshu, was home for the festival holidays from Mumbai, where he runs a small manufacturing unit that makes nuts, bolts and fasteners for local industries. Sudhanshu is the kind of manufacturer I enjoy meeting.

He knows his machines, he knows his customers by name, and he is tired of spending his evenings typing invoices and answering the same order questions on the phone. Within an hour of lunch, he had shown us how his unit runs and asked how much of the customer communication, invoicing and order workflow could run without adding staff.

We have started working with him on exactly that, and I will write about the business side another time.

The conversation that matters for this article started after lunch, on a charpai under a neem tree at the edge of the field. Sudhanshu, like many small business owners with a little surplus, had started putting some savings into American shares through an app that lets you invest in US stocks from India.

He had seen a television programme the previous week that worried him. He played it for us on his phone while a buffalo watched from the tube well.

It was a [Zee Business programme on Stockal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-pXHp6MwBYg), part of a series in which the channel raised questions about the platform. The programme asked how investors' US shares could be sold without their permission, why low-volume Chinese shares were bought, and why the risk management system did not stop it. Another episode in the same series asked how transactions worth large sums could happen without an OTP.

## What the Stockal complaints are, and what they are not

I want to be careful here, because Sudhanshu wanted me to be careful too. The questions raised in these programmes are allegations and investor concerns being reported by a news channel, and the company has its own account of events, which I have not covered. I am not saying any particular person has done anything wrong, and I would ask readers to treat stockal complaints as questions to verify, not verdicts.

What I am saying is that Stockal complaints, as reported, describe exactly the kind of risk that most Indian business owners never think about when they download a foreign stocks app india offers through advertisements. They assume that because the app is slick, the shares are listed on a famous American exchange, and the payment went through their own bank, everything is protected.

Sahadev, who has the consultant's habit of turning every worry into a framework, put it plainly. "There are three things," he said. "Where is the money legally allowed to go, who actually holds the shares, and who answers when something goes wrong. Most people check none of them."

Kaka, who had never bought a share in his life, listened with great interest and then said that this is like a pilgrim who gives his bag to a man at the station who promises to carry it to the temple. "The bag is still his bag," he said. "But he does not know the man's name."

## The legal route: LRS limit for investment, TCS on foreign remittance and overseas investment rules India sets

The first of Sahadev's three questions is the easiest to answer. For a resident individual, money for investing abroad usually goes out under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme of the [Reserve Bank of India](https://www.rbi.org.in/), which allows remittances up to a set annual limit per person for permitted purposes, including buying shares listed overseas.

[IMAGE 3]

The LRS limit for investment is per person per financial year, and every remittance is routed through an authorised bank, which reports it. When you use an app, the app's partner bank or your own bank sends the money under LRS on your behalf.

That means your PAN and your bank are on record for every rupee that leaves, which is good for transparency. The overseas investment rules India follows also include tax collection at source on certain foreign remittances.

TCS on foreign remittance has changed more than once in recent budgets, with thresholds and rates depending on the purpose, so I told Sudhanshu to check the current rules with his chartered accountant rather than trust any app's summary.

TCS can usually be adjusted against your income tax, but it affects cash flow, which matters for a business owner moving money in and out. None of this is complicated once you know it, and none of it, on its own, protects you from a platform problem. LRS tells you the money left legally. It does not tell you where your shares are sitting.

## Custody of US shares and US stock broker registration, the questions nobody asks

This is the part where Sudhanshu leaned forward and stopped scrolling. When you buy an American share through an Indian app, the app is usually not the broker that holds your shares. The app is often an interface, and the actual custody of US shares sits with a US-registered broker-dealer that the app partners with, under an account in your name or in an omnibus structure.

[IMAGE 4]

That difference matters enormously. If your shares are held in an account in your own name at a registered US broker, you can usually see that account, receive statements from that broker and, in a dispute, approach that broker and its regulator directly.

If the structure is less clear, you depend far more on the app. US stock broker registration can be checked publicly.

Ask the app, in writing, for the exact legal name of the US broker that holds your shares, then search that name on the US regulator's broker check tool, and confirm that statements come from that broker, not only from the app. Sahadev added a fourth check that I liked: ask whether the app can place or cancel trades in your account without your instruction, and under what written conditions.

Kaka, still thinking about his pilgrim's bag, asked whether there is any way to know your bag is safe while it is in the other man's hand. "Yes," Sahadev said. "You ask for a receipt from the temple, not from the porter." Sahadev then explained the two common structures in plain words for his brother.

In one, each investor has an individual account at the US broker, opened through the app, with the investor's own name on it. In the other, the app or its partner holds a pooled account for many investors and keeps its own internal record of who owns what.

The pooled structure is not automatically wrong, but it means the investor relies more on the app's records and controls, which is exactly where the questions in the television programme were aimed.

There is also a newer route that some platforms use, through entities set up in the GIFT City international financial centre in Gujarat, which are overseen by an Indian regulator for that centre. For an investor, this can mean an Indian complaint channel exists, which is worth knowing before you choose.

Whichever structure an app uses, the duty to ask is the investor's, and the duty to answer clearly is the platform's. Sudhanshu said that in his factory, every bag of bolts that leaves carries a delivery challan with the customer's name, the quantity and a signature.

"If I cannot ship a bag of bolts without a paper," he said, "how did I send my savings without asking for one?" That one question, I told him, is worth more than any app review he could read.

## Four checks before you invest in US stocks from India

On the charpai, with the sun dropping toward the mustard fields, we wrote down the checks Sudhanshu now wants every friend of his to run.

I am sharing the table as we wrote it, because it is short enough to send on any family group.

| Check | Where to verify it | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the US broker that holds my shares | Ask the app in writing, then search the broker's legal name on the US regulator's broker check | A named, registered broker-dealer with your account in your own name |
| Do I receive statements from that broker directly | Your email and the broker's own portal | Regular statements from the broker, not only screenshots in the app |
| Can anyone trade in my account without my instruction | The app's terms and your written confirmation from support | No trades without your authorisation, with OTP or similar confirmation |
| How is my money sent and taxed | Your bank's LRS records and your chartered accountant | Remittance under LRS through an authorised bank, TCS understood in advance |

Sudhanshu looked at the third row for a long time. "That is the one," he said. "If they can sell without asking, the other three do not help." Sahadev said that the table is also a good test of the company itself.

A trustworthy foreign stocks app india investors use will answer these four questions clearly and quickly, in writing. An evasive answer to any of them is itself an answer.

## When withdrawal from foreign stock app accounts takes too long

Sudhanshu's real worry was not theoretical. A friend of his in Mumbai had tried to withdraw money from a foreign stock app and been told it would take "a few more working days" for weeks.

I told him what I would do, step by step, for any withdrawal from foreign stock app accounts that stalls. First, take dated screenshots of your holdings, your transaction history and every withdrawal request, and download every statement you can.

Second, write to the app's support by email, not only chat, asking for the status, the reason for delay, and the name of the US broker processing the withdrawal.

Third, write directly to that US broker with your account details if you have an account in your own name. Fourth, if the app is regulated in India in any capacity, for example through a GIFT City entity, check which Indian regulator oversees it and use that regulator's complaint channel.

Fifth, keep your bank and your chartered accountant informed, because money stuck abroad still has tax consequences. And finally, if a programme or a group of investors is already raising the issue, join them, because collective complaints are heard faster. Sahadev added one more, from his consulting days. "Never send more money in to get money out," he said.

"That rule is true for foreign apps, for local schemes, for everything." It is the same rule I wrote about in [the fake trading app that shows profits you cannot withdraw](https://ethicalfounder.in/fake-trading-app-profits-cant-withdraw/), and it holds here too.

## Questions People Ask Before They Invest in US Stocks From India

**How can I invest in US stocks from India?**

Resident individuals can invest in US stocks from India by sending money abroad under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme through an authorised bank, usually via an app or broker that partners with a registered US broker-dealer.

**What is the LRS limit for investment abroad?**

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme allows resident individuals to remit up to a set annual limit per person per financial year for permitted purposes, including buying overseas shares, and the current limit should be confirmed on the RBI website.

**How is TCS on foreign remittance applied to share investments?**

Tax collection at source may apply to certain foreign remittances above a threshold, and the rates and rules have changed over time, so check the current position with your chartered accountant.

**Who holds my shares when I use a foreign stocks app in India?**

Usually a US-registered broker-dealer that partners with the app holds the shares, and you should ask the app in writing for that broker's legal name and confirm your account details with the broker.

**How do I check US stock broker registration?**

Ask for the exact legal name of the broker that holds your shares and search it on the US regulator's public broker check tool, then confirm that you receive statements from that broker.

**What are the Stockal complaints about?**

A Zee Business programme series raised investor concerns about the Stockal platform, including questions about shares being sold without permission and transactions without OTP; these are reported allegations, and the company has its own account.

**What should I do if withdrawal from a foreign stock app is delayed?**

Save screenshots and statements, write to the app and the holding broker by email, check which regulator oversees the app's entity, keep your bank and accountant informed, and never pay extra to release funds.

**What are the overseas investment rules in India for individuals?**

Individuals invest abroad mainly under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, through authorised banks that report each remittance, subject to the annual limit and applicable tax collection at source.

**Is global investing for business owners a good idea?**

It can be, after the business has a cash buffer, the owner keeps business and personal money separate, and the owner understands the product and its custody arrangements well enough to explain them simply.

**What is custody of US shares and why does it matter?**

Custody means who legally holds your shares; when they sit in an account in your own name with a registered broker, you have clearer rights and a direct path if something goes wrong.
