# A monthly profit partnership in a sand business sounded safe, until an Asian Games gold medallist went to the police

## Morning tea on the second train

We had changed trains at dawn, and by the time the sun cleared the fields the second coach woke up all at once.

[IMAGE 1]

Curtains were pulled back, toothbrushes were carried down the aisle, and somebody's phone began playing the Suprabhatam at a volume that suggested the owner was still half asleep.

The retired soldier from the night before had made the same change, found a seat two bays away and was reading his newspaper with great seriousness, while Shankar Kaka slept sitting up, the way only guides can.

Across the aisle sat a couple from Vadodara who had boarded the same train. The husband was a quiet civil contractor who builds small houses and shop rows in the city outskirts. The wife coaches girls in athletics at a district sports hostel, and she had the strong forearms and the habit of checking her watch that coaches seem to carry everywhere.

They were going to Kashi to perform a ritual for her late father, and she had the ashes in a small copper pot wrapped in red cloth, kept carefully in her lap.

I offered them the extra tea the attendant had left, and the conversation began, as conversations in trains do, with food and family. It moved to money only when the husband looked at my notebook and asked what I was writing.

I told him about the bot story from the night before. His wife put down her cup and said, "Have you read what happened to Sarita didi?" She did not mean a relative.

She meant Sarita Gaikwad, the Gujarat athlete who won gold for India at the Asian Games, a name that every young girl in her hostel knows. "She is the reason half my girls started running," she said.

"And now she is in the news for this." She opened her phone and showed me the report.

## A gold medallist, a monthly promise and the Sarita Gaikwad fraud case

The report she showed me was from [Aaj Tak's Gujarat desk](https://www.aajtak.in/amp/india/gujarat/story/asian-games-gold-medalist-sarita-gaikwad-69-lakh-investment-fraud-gujarat-lcla-strc-2688048-2026-11-04), filed from Ahmedabad. According to that report, Sarita Gaikwad has alleged that she lost ₹69.10 lakh after a man named Nitin Bharti persuaded her to put money into a sand business.

The promise, as she has described it to police, was a return of up to four percent every month on the money invested, and the return of the principal within one year. She transferred the money online.

When the time came to ask for it back, she received excuses, and eventually she went to the police.

Another Hindi report I read on the same morning put the figure at ₹67.30 lakh and described her as a DSP, which is the post she holds in the Gujarat Police. Figures in early reports often differ by a few lakh as complaints are refined, and I will keep to the report I have linked.

Everything in this case is still at the stage of a complaint and an allegation, and the accused has his own side to present in court.

I want to be clear about that, because the point of this article is the structure of the offer, not the guilt of any one man.

The coach sat with her phone in her hands for a moment after I returned it. "She is a police officer," she said.

"She runs for the country. If she can be pulled in, what about my girls' parents?" That question is the reason I began writing this piece in the moving train itself, on the back of a ticket envelope, and finished it later the same evening on the ghats. The sarita gaikwad fraud case is being discussed in sports hostels and police canteens across Gujarat for exactly that reason.

People assume that fraud happens to the careless. This case shows that a monthly profit partnership can be sold to people who are disciplined, brave and intelligent, because it does not ask them to be careless. It asks them to trust.

Shankar Kaka, who had woken by then and was peeling an orange for everyone, said something I wrote down at once.

"Bholenath is easy to please," he said, using the name by which so many devotees call Lord Shiva, "but he is never easy to fool. People should be like that with money too." The coach laughed for the first time that morning, and then said that she would put that line on the notice board of her hostel.

## Why no monthly profit partnership can promise a 4 percent monthly return

The husband had been listening without speaking, and now he leaned forward. "Four percent a month on sand," he said slowly, as if tasting the number.

"Forty-eight percent a year. I have bought sand for every house I have built and I have never seen a supplier who makes that." I asked him to explain what he meant, and he did, with the patience of a man who has explained the same thing to many clients.

A sand business does make money. In many states, the trade in river sand and manufactured sand is a large business, tied to the construction cycle. But its margin moves with the season, with mining permissions, with transport costs, with diesel prices, with the monsoon, and with the pace of building in the nearby towns. In the rainy months, river mining slows or stops in many places.

When a large project in the area finishes, demand falls. When fuel prices rise, as I have written about in [what fuel price changes do to small businesses](https://ethicalfounder.in/iran-war-fuel-prices-small-businesses-india/), the transport cost of every truckload changes overnight. A business with these swings can share profit. It cannot promise a fixed profit every month without either lying or paying that profit from somewhere other than the business.

That is the heart of every monthly profit partnership scheme I have looked at. The word "partnership" suggests that you share in the ups and downs. The word "monthly" with a fixed percentage tells you there are no downs. Both cannot be true at the same time.

A 4 percent monthly return works like an interest rate, and a very high one, and when someone offers you an interest rate while calling it a partnership, it is fair to ask why he does not simply borrow from a bank at a far lower rate.

The answer, in most of these cases, is that no bank would lend to that business on those terms, or that there is no business of that size at all.

## What a sand business investment actually earns

The husband took my notebook and drew a small table in it, which I am reproducing in my own words because it was the clearest thing anybody said all morning. He buys sand by the truckload from suppliers for his projects.

[IMAGE 2]

The supplier's earning on each load comes from the difference between what he pays at the source, including royalties, loading and transport, and what the builder pays at the site.

On a good month, that difference covers his trucks, his drivers, his permits and gives him a profit he can live on. On a bad month, he may run at a loss to keep his trucks moving and his drivers paid.

"If he takes ₹50 lakh from an investor," the husband said, "he must make ₹2 lakh a month extra profit just to pay her four percent. That is many trucks. Where are those trucks?"

I have seen the same question asked in a different form in my article on [what retail margins really look like in a small-town pharmacy](https://ethicalfounder.in/small-town-pharmacy-business-lessons-retail-margins/).

Every business has a realistic margin, and anyone who promises you more than that margin is promising you something the business cannot produce. For a sand business investment, the honest calculation starts with trucks, trips, permits and seasons.

For a construction business investment, it starts with projects, payment cycles and how long builders take to pay. In both cases, the realistic return to an outside investor is uneven, seasonal and sometimes zero for months. That is the nature of business. A promoter who cannot tell you about the bad months has either never run the business or does not want you to know.

Kaka added his own example from Kashi, where the Ganga lays down wide sandbanks on the far side of the river every year. "Every monsoon the river moves the sand," he said.

"One year the bank is near the ghats, next year it is far. The boatmen who carry people across never say the sand will be in the same place next year. Only a man who has never been on the river would promise that." The husband laughed and said he would use that line with his own clients.

## The partnership deed India investors skip most often

The coach had a question that I think many readers will share. "If she had a written paper," she asked, "would it have helped?" It would have helped, though it would not have guaranteed anything.

[IMAGE 3]

In India, a partnership is governed by the Indian Partnership Act, and a partnership deed india investors sign is the document that sets out who the partners are, what each contributes, how profit and loss are shared, who manages the business and what happens when someone wants to leave.

Many people who put money into a friend's or acquaintance's business skip this entirely. They transfer money on trust, perhaps with a few WhatsApp messages, and they believe the friendship is the contract. I understand why.

In our culture, asking for papers can feel like an insult, as if you are calling the other person a thief. But a written investment agreement protects both people. It protects the investor from a partner who disappears, and it protects the honest businessman from an investor who demands money back during a bad season.

I have written before about [the difference between a side income and a registered business](https://ethicalfounder.in/side-income-vs-registered-business/), and the same thinking applies when you are on the investing side.

If the business you are putting money into is not registered, has no bank account in its own name, files no tax returns and has no deed, then you are not a partner.

You are a lender without security, and you are lending to a person, not to a business. Before any partnership, I also check whether the business exists on paper at all.

If it is a company or an LLP, its name, directors and filings can be searched on the [Ministry of Corporate Affairs portal](https://www.mca.gov.in/), and that search takes ten minutes. If it is a sole proprietorship, ask for the GST registration, the bank statements of the business account and the trade licence. If it is only a person with a story, then the story is all you are buying.

Kaka told us that when he started his own guide work in Kashi with a partner, both of them wrote everything down on a single page and had it signed at a notary near the court, even though they were close friends.

"We are still friends," he said, "because the paper did the arguing for us when we disagreed." I have heard many lawyers explain the value of a deed, and none of them put it better than that.

## Profit sharing agreement clauses that decide whether money comes back

The husband asked me to write down the clauses I would insist on, and I did so in the margin of his newspaper while the train crossed a long bridge over a river that was low and brown at this time of year.

[IMAGE 5]

Here they are, in a table, because a table is easier to carry to a family meeting than a paragraph.

| Clause in a profit sharing agreement | What it protects |
|---|---|
| Names, addresses and ID of every partner | You know exactly who you are dealing with, and so does a court. |
| Capital contributed by each partner, with bank transfer details | Proves what you put in, and that it went to the business account, not a personal one. |
| How profit and loss are shared, as a ratio, not a fixed amount | Makes it a real partnership and removes the fixed monthly promise. |
| Right to see books and bank statements every quarter | Lets you check that the business exists and earns what is claimed. |
| Who signs cheques and approves large payments | Stops one partner from moving money out alone. |
| Exit terms: notice period and how your share is valued | Tells you how and when you can leave and what you will receive. |
| Dispute resolution: arbitration or jurisdiction | Gives you a defined path if things go wrong. |
| Registration of the deed and stamp duty paid as per your state | Makes the document stronger as evidence. |

A profit sharing agreement with these clauses will not stop a determined cheat. But it will make the cheat's work harder, it will give you evidence, and it will often make a dishonest person walk away before taking your money, because he does not want paperwork that can be used against him.

That reaction is itself useful information. If someone resists every one of these clauses, he has answered your question about him. As I finished the table, Kaka reminded us of King Harishchandra, whose name is carried by the ghat where I later finished writing this piece.

In the story every child in Kashi knows, the king gave up his kingdom and his comfort rather than break his word, and ended up serving at the cremation ground itself.

"He did not need a deed," Kaka said, "because his word was a deed. Most people today are not Harishchandra, so they should sign." The coach and her husband both folded their hands when he said the king's name, and so did I.

## How to check a construction business investment before you pay

The coach's girls' parents were on her mind, and she asked me for something short she could share in the hostel parents' group. I gave her five checks. First, visit the business.

Go to the actual site rather than the office: the sand yard, the trucks, the stockpile, the weighbridge. A real sand business is loud, dusty and full of people.

Second, speak to two customers who buy from the business, chosen by you, not by the promoter. Third, ask to see six months of the business bank account statement, and look for regular payments from builders and regular payments to transporters.

Fourth, ask the promoter why he needs your money and not a bank's. Listen carefully to the answer. Fifth, put any money you invest only into the business's own bank account, never into a personal account, and only after the deed is signed.

These checks apply to investing in a friends business just as much as to investing with a stranger. Perhaps more, because with a friend we tend to skip them.

I have also learnt, from many founders I have spoken with over the years, that the honest businessman usually welcomes these checks. He is proud of his yard and his trucks. He likes showing them.

It is the dishonest one who becomes offended. The retired soldier, who had walked over from his bay to borrow a pen, listened to this part and agreed strongly. "In the army we inspect before we trust," he said.

"Not because we doubt the man. Because we respect the job." I added that to my list, because it is the best answer to the guilt people feel when they ask for papers from someone they know.

For readers who are themselves running a small enterprise and thinking of bringing in money from relatives, I would gently suggest reading my notes on [testing a business idea before spending](https://ethicalfounder.in/business-ideas-from-a-family-complaint-test-before-spending/), because the same discipline that protects an investor also protects the person who takes the investment.

## Brochures that look better than the business

Later that morning, the coach showed me a forwarded PDF that had reached her hostel parents' group the week before. It was an offer for a different sand and aggregates venture, unrelated to the case in the news, promising a fixed monthly share to small investors. The brochure was beautiful.

It had photographs of gleaming yards, smiling workers, a fleet of trucks in a neat row, and a profit chart that rose steadily every month.

The husband looked at it for about thirty seconds and laughed. "These trucks are too clean," he said.

"No sand truck in India is this clean." He was right, and when I looked closely, the images had the soft, perfect texture of pictures made with image-generation tools. This is the AI side of the monthly profit partnership problem that very few people talk about.

It now costs almost nothing to produce a brochure that looks like it came from a large company, complete with yard photographs, a team page and even a short video of a "director" speaking.

I have written about how sellers use [AI product images for Amazon and Flipkart](https://ethicalfounder.in/ai-product-images-for-amazon-and-flipkart/), and the same tools that help an honest seller can help a dishonest promoter dress up a business that does not exist.

India has begun to respond with rules on synthetic content, which I covered in my note on [AI labelling rules under the IT Rules amendment](https://ethicalfounder.in/ai-labelling-rules-in-india-it-rules-amendment/), but a forwarded PDF on a family group will rarely carry any label.

So the investor must treat every polished brochure as an advertisement and every photograph as a claim to be verified on site.

A partnership deed india investors sign should refer to real assets you have seen with your own eyes: the yard, the trucks, the registration papers, the bank account.

If the only proof is a brochure, then the brochure is the business, and that is not something you can recover money from.

## What the contractor does with his own surplus

I asked the husband a question I ask every honest businessman I meet: where do you keep your own extra money? He smiled, as if he had been waiting to be asked. He keeps three buckets, he said.

The first is a working buffer in the firm's current account, enough to pay labour and suppliers for two months even if no client pays on time.

Clients often pay late in construction, and he has started using simple reminders of the kind I described in [late payment automation for small firms](https://ethicalfounder.in/late-payment-automation/), which he said has cut his follow-up calls in half.

The second bucket is fixed deposits and a few mutual funds through his bank, which he does not touch except for emergencies.

The third is his own projects: a small plot, a shop row he is building to rent out, things he can see and control. "I never put money where I cannot stand," he said. That is a sound principle for any small business owner.

He also mentioned that he keeps his personal and business taxes clean, because a clean record makes bank loans easy, and a bank loan at a fair rate removes the temptation to borrow from or lend to informal schemes.

For self-employed readers, the details of presumptive taxation are worth understanding, and I have explained them in [Section 58 of the Income Tax Act 2025 for freelancers and small earners](https://ethicalfounder.in/section-58-income-tax-act-2025-presumptive-tax-freelancers/), along with [how a freelancer's tax identity differs from a business owner's](https://ethicalfounder.in/freelancer-vs-business-owner-tax-identity-difference/).

Investing in a friends business, he added, is something he does only in the third bucket, and only for amounts he could lose without changing his family's life.

He keeps a simple daily note of cash in and cash out, the kind of habit I describe in [daily business reporting](https://ethicalfounder.in/daily-business-reporting-automation/), and he said that note is what tells him when he has a real surplus and when he only feels rich. I wrote that down. It is better advice than most of what is sold as financial planning.

And for readers in smaller places who want their surplus to build something real, there are steady ventures worth studying in my list of [business ideas for small towns](https://ethicalfounder.in/business-ideas-for-small-towns/), where the money stays close enough to stand on.

## Why careful people still say yes to investing in a friends business

There was a long halt at a junction around noon, and the platform smelled of hot samosas and diesel. The husband went to buy water, and the coach and I continued talking. She asked the question I hear in almost every conversation about fraud: why did a smart person agree?

[IMAGE 4]

I told her what I believe, which is that most people who fall into a monthly profit partnership are not attracted by greed alone.

They are attracted by a combination of three things. The first is relationship. The promoter is often someone known, sometimes for years, someone who has eaten at your table or helped you in a difficult time. The second is plausibility.

Sand, construction, real estate, transport: these are real businesses that everyone can see growing around them, so the story fits the world they see. The third is relief.

For a person with savings and a busy life, the promise that the money will grow on its own every month, without stock markets or apps, feels like a relief from having to think about it.

That third pull is the most dangerous, because it is the one we do not notice. Sarita Gaikwad trains, travels, serves in the police and carries the expectations of a state.

I can easily imagine why a simple monthly arrangement with someone she trusted would feel like one less thing to manage. I say that with respect, and with no claim to know her mind, only from what I have seen in many people. The coach was quiet for a while. "My father also gave money like this once," she said.

"To his cousin. For a brick kiln. He never got it back and he never complained. He said it was his karma." She touched the red cloth around the copper pot when she said it.

## Business partner cheating and saying no to a trusted person

The hardest part of all this advice is the moment when the person asking for money is someone you love or respect.

Business partner cheating is painful because it begins with closeness, and saying no can feel like ending a relationship.

I gave the coach a few lines that I have used myself, and that I have seen work in my own family. "I will invest only through a registered deed and into the business account, so that both of us are protected."

"Let me come and see the yard first, and then we can talk about amounts." "I keep my savings in fixed places for my family's needs, but I am happy to help you find a bank loan for the business." None of these lines accuse anybody.

All of them put the burden of proof where it belongs. If the relationship cannot survive a request for papers, it was carrying a weight it was never built for.

For small business owners who are on the other side, raising money for their own work, I would suggest the same honesty in reverse. Offer the deed before you are asked. Show the books. Explain the bad months.

I have written about [managing money in the first six months of a small business](https://ethicalfounder.in/money-in-the-first-six-months-of-a-small-business/), and the founders who do best in that period are the ones who are honest with their backers about how uneven early income can be.

## Lakshmi, effort and a monthly profit partnership that is earned

The husband came back with three bottles of water and a newspaper of his own. As the train pulled out of that junction, his wife began to quietly recite the Vishnu Sahasranama, and he joined her in a low voice, and for a few minutes the coach seemed to slow down even though the train was speeding up.

When they finished, the husband said something that has stayed with me through the afternoon. "Lakshmi stays where there is effort," he said.

"She visits where there is luck. She leaves where there is cheating." In my own understanding of karma, there is a difference between the fruit of work and the fruit of a promise. The fruit of work is uneven, seasonal and sometimes disappointing, like the sand business itself. But it is real, and it belongs to the one who worked. The fruit of a promise, when the promise is false, is borrowed from someone else's loss.

Shankar Kaka, who had woken by then, said quietly that on the burning ghats of Kashi every person learns that only the fruit of honest action walks with us to the end, which is the teaching Lord Shiva is said to show at Harishchandra Ghat every single day.

The Gita tells us that we have a right to our action and not to its fruit, and I have always read that as practical advice as much as spiritual advice.

A monthly profit partnership that promises the fruit without the uncertainty of the action is offering something that, in that understanding, nobody can honestly offer. I am not the victim here, and neither are the coach and her husband.

They are people who know a woman who brought pride to their state, and who now feel that her loss is a warning to every family in their hostel. I hope she recovers her money through the courts, and I hope the case is heard fairly for everyone involved.

The train seemed to have become a place where people bring the stories they have been carrying, and Kashi, I have learnt, does the same.

## Questions People Ask About Monthly Profit Partnership

**What is a monthly profit partnership?**

It is an arrangement where an investor gives money to a business or person in return for a fixed share every month, often described as a partnership but working like a high-interest loan with no security.

**Can a sand business investment really pay 4 percent a month?**

A real sand business has seasonal and uneven margins, so a fixed 4 percent monthly return, which comes to about 48 percent a year, is far above what such a business can reliably produce.

**What happened in the Sarita Gaikwad fraud case?**

According to an Aaj Tak report, the Asian Games gold medallist alleged that a man promised her up to four percent monthly profit and the principal back within a year for investing in a sand business, and that she lost ₹69.10 lakh before approaching police.

**Is a partnership deed in India mandatory?**

A partnership can exist without a written deed, but a written and registered partnership deed is strongly advisable because it records contributions, profit sharing and exit terms and serves as evidence in any dispute.

**What should a written investment agreement include?**

It should name every partner, record the capital and how it was paid, set out profit and loss sharing as a ratio, give the right to inspect books, define signing authority, explain exit terms and specify how disputes are resolved.

**How do I check a construction business investment before paying?**

Visit the actual site, speak to customers you choose, review six months of the business bank account, check registrations such as GST and company records, and pay only into the business account after signing a deed.

**Is investing in a friend's business safe?**

It can be, if you treat it like any other investment with a deed, inspection rights and payment only to the business account, and riskier if you rely on trust alone.

**What are the signs of business partner cheating?**

Common signs include a fixed monthly promise, reluctance to give papers or show books, requests to pay into a personal account, and excuses or delays when you ask for returns or your principal.

**What is a profit sharing agreement?**

It is a contract that sets out how the profits and losses of a business are divided among partners or investors, usually as a percentage of actual profit rather than a fixed amount.

**What can I do if my money is stuck in a monthly profit partnership?**

Collect all transfer records and messages, send a written demand, consult a lawyer, and file a police complaint for cheating if there is evidence of a false promise from the start.
