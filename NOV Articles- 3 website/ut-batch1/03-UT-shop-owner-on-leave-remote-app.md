# Shop owner on leave: how a Bengaluru supermarket owner runs his store, his eleven staff and his CCTV from one app in his own voice

## The owner who never went to his own village

When I travel during Diwali, I meet many shop owners who haven't taken a single proper holiday in years. They tell me this with a strange kind of pride, and then, after the second tea, with sadness. "Who will look after the shop?" they ask. "If I go for three days, everything falls apart."

This Diwali, in one shop, the owner told me he hadn't attended a cousin's wedding in his own village because Saturday is his busiest day. In another, the owner said his wife plans every family trip knowing he will come for one day and leave the next morning. A shop owner on leave, for most of them, is a dream they gave up long ago.

[IMAGE 1]

I listened, I joked, and I asked my usual question: "Uncle, the world is updating. Why can't you try something so the shop speaks in your voice even when you are not there?" They laughed. A shop that speaks? But when one of them asked how, I told him about Sudarshan Rao.

## Sudarshan Rao, a supermarket in Bengaluru and eleven staff

Sudarshan Rao is originally from Andhra Pradesh and runs a supermarket in Bengaluru under an Indian franchise. I won't name the franchise or his store, because those details are his to share. What I can tell you is that his store is busy from morning to night, with eleven staff members across billing, shelves, stock, delivery and cleaning.

When I first met him, his day looked like most owners' days. He arrived before opening and left after closing. He gave the same instructions every morning. He answered staff questions all day, walked the floor to check shelves, watched the CCTV screen between customers, and handled supplier calls in between. If he stepped out for an hour, his phone rang ten times. If he went to Andhra Pradesh for a family function, he spent the whole function on the phone.

I did what I always do. I didn't sell anything. I sat with him, I made fun of his habit of checking the CCTV even while talking to me, and I asked him: "Rao garu, why can't the store hear you even when you are not there?" He asked what I meant. I asked him to call someone from his family who was comfortable with technology. A younger member of his family came the next day. I explained the idea to him, gave Rao garu my card and left.

What we built afterwards is the most complete system we have made for any shop so far, and today his entire family uses it. Let me walk you through it.

## The base: an AI shop assistant with the owner's knowledge

The foundation of Sudarshan Rao's system is the same idea we used in an earlier project, an [AI shop assistant in the owner's voice](https://upcomingtools.com/ai-shop-assistant-owner-voice-staff-training/). It holds everything Rao garu knows about the store: product locations, prices, offers, supplier timings, store rules and the way he wants customers treated.

On top of that base, we added an AI coding agent that maintains the system with two kinds of memory. Permanent memory holds things that don't change: store rules, staff roles, product categories, the opening routine. Temporary memory holds things that change daily or weekly: today's offers, this week's festival display, a supplier delay, a staff member on leave. The agent keeps both in order, so the store always knows what is permanent and what is only for now.

This difference matters more than people think. In most shops, temporary instructions get mixed with permanent ones, and staff forget which is which. "Is that offer still on?" "Did anna say that only for Sunday?" With two memories, there is no confusion.

## An owner voice clone that speaks through an ai sound box for shop aisles

The heart of the system is Rao garu's voice. With his clear consent, we created an owner voice clone from his own recordings. Every announcement, every instruction and every reminder in the store is spoken in his voice through a store-wide AI sound box for shop use: a set of speakers placed in the billing area, the aisles, the stock room and the staff area.

Why his voice and not a neutral one? Because staff respond to the owner's voice differently. When Rao garu's voice says "Billing counter two, please open, the queue is growing," it gets done. When a recorded machine voice says the same thing, people wait to see if anyone else will do it. His family tested both, and the difference was obvious within a day.

[IMAGE 2]

We also made the sound box speak in more than one language, because his staff come from different states. Telugu, Kannada, Hindi and simple English. For anyone curious about how far Indian-language voice technology has come, the government's [Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/) initiative is a good place to start reading.

## The remote shop management app: one place for staff, store, payments and CCTV

Everything Rao garu needs lives in one remote shop management app on his phone. I insisted on one app, because owners already juggle too many. Staff, store, payments and CCTV, all in one place, with large buttons that he can use while walking, eating or sitting at a family function.

When he opens the app, he sees four things. His staff, listed by name, with who is on duty today. His store, with a live CCTV view of the main areas. His payments, with the day's sales so far. And his instructions, the ones running today and the ones saved for later.

[IMAGE 3]

The design rule was simple: Rao garu should be able to run his store with a few taps. He watches the CCTV from anywhere, and when he sees something that needs attention, he taps A, B, C or D. A for a pre-made instruction, B for a mood-based message, C for a voice recording, D to call a staff member directly. That's it. No long menus, no settings to hunt for.

## Staff names and mood buttons in the staff announcement system

This is the part of the app that makes everyone laugh when I explain it, and then makes them think. Every staff member is listed by name. Rao garu can tap one name, several names, or "everyone." Then he chooses a mood.

There are mood buttons: polite, firm, appreciative, urgent, angry and a few more. Each mood has about ten pre-written options in his voice, ready to play. Under "appreciative," for example, there are lines like "Very good work on the shelves today," and "Thank you for handling that customer patiently." Under "firm," there are lines like "Please don't leave the counter empty," and "The cold storage door must stay closed."

[IMAGE 4]

So if Rao garu sees on the CCTV that four staff members are standing together near the stock room at lunchtime, he taps their four names, taps "polite" and chooses "lunch," and the sound box says, in his voice: "Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, Shatrughna, you four go for lunch now, others will cover." I use those four names when I explain this to other owners, because everyone remembers them, and because it always gets a smile.

The angry button exists, and Rao garu says he uses it less than he expected. He told me that the moment staff knew he could speak to them by name at any time, the need for anger mostly disappeared. The staff announcement system made the store feel watched in a fair way, with the owner's voice present even when he wasn't.

He can also edit any of these lines at any time from the app. If a line doesn't sound like him, he changes it. If he wants a new line for a new situation, he adds it. The system is his voice, written his way.

## Hold to record: shop staff instructions that are permanent, temporary or repeating

Pre-written lines can't cover everything. So the app has one big button: hold to record. Rao garu holds it, speaks his instruction, and lets go. Then he chooses two things.

First, is this instruction permanent or temporary? A permanent one, like a new rule for handling returns, goes into the store's permanent memory and becomes part of the daily routine. A temporary one, like a supplier coming late today, goes into temporary memory and is removed automatically when it expires.

Second, how often should it play? Once, daily, weekly, hourly or every fifteen minutes. For Diwali, Rao garu recorded a festival greeting for customers, "Happy Diwali from all of us, please check our festival offers near the entrance," and set it to play every fifteen minutes during store hours for the festival week. Then it disappeared on its own.

[IMAGE 5]

These shop staff instructions used to take Rao garu half an hour every morning, standing in front of the staff, repeating himself. Now the morning briefing plays automatically when the store opens, in his voice, with the permanent routine first and today's temporary instructions after.

## CCTV with speaker for shop floors

Many shops already have CCTV. Very few use it for anything except checking what went wrong after it went wrong. In Rao garu's store, we connected the cameras to the same app and paired each area with a CCTV with speaker for shop zones, so he can see a place and speak to it at the same time.

When he sees a customer waiting at an empty counter, he taps that zone and the speaker in that area says, in his voice, "One moment, sir, someone is coming to help you," and the staff hear it too. When he sees spilled liquid in an aisle, he taps the cleaning staff's name and "urgent." When a delivery van arrives at the back door, he taps the stock room and says, "Delivery has arrived, please receive it."

I want to be careful here, because CCTV and speakers raise questions of privacy and dignity. We designed the system so that cameras cover only work areas, never staff rest areas or washrooms. Staff were told clearly what the cameras and speakers do. And the speaker is used for instructions and appreciation, never for shouting at anyone in front of customers. Rao garu agreed with every one of these rules before we built anything.

## Using the ai sound box for shop customers, gently

The sound box was built for staff, but customers hear it too, so we designed it with them in mind. During store hours, staff instructions play at a low volume in the staff areas, while customer announcements, like festival greetings and offer reminders, play softly across the aisles.

We kept customer announcements short and rare. Nobody likes a store that shouts at them every two minutes. A greeting when the store opens, an offer reminder a few times a day, a polite closing announcement in the evening and a festival message during festival weeks. For planning those seasonal messages, I often use the ideas in my [local festival calendar for business ideas](https://upcomingtools.com/business-ideas-around-festivals-local-festival-calendar/).

Customers who know Rao garu sometimes smile when they hear his voice in the aisles. One regular reportedly asked the billing staff whether "sir is hiding somewhere." That small moment of familiarity is part of what keeps a neighbourhood store alive, the same [neighbourhood reputation](https://upcomingtools.com/business-ideas-using-neighborhood-reputation/) that no franchise logo can buy.

## What a remote shop management app should never do

Every tool can be misused, and I tell every owner this before we build anything. A remote shop management app gives the owner a lot of power over his staff, and that power must be used with respect.

It should never be used to embarrass a staff member in front of customers. It should never be used to watch staff in private areas. It should never replace a real conversation when something serious goes wrong. If a staff member is struggling, the owner should call him and talk, or meet him in person, and keep the speakers out of it.

Rao garu understood this immediately. He told me that his staff are like family, and the app is only a way to stay present with them. That attitude is why the system works in his store. For owners who want a gentler way for staff to raise their own questions, my notes on a [staff helpdesk chatbot that answers voice notes](https://upcomingtools.com/hr-helpdesk-chatbot-on-telegram-voice-note-support/) offer another idea.

## Staff training videos and regional-language audio

With eleven staff from different backgrounds, training was always a problem. New staff learned by watching old staff, and old staff had their own habits, some good, some not. So we created a set of staff training videos and audio lessons in Rao garu's voice, in the regional languages his staff speak.

The videos show the store's way of doing things: arranging shelves, handling billing, receiving deliveries, dealing with a difficult customer, closing the store at night. The audio lessons are shorter, for staff to listen to on the way to work. We made them with a one-time audio setup, and the system runs on a monthly subscription that covers updates and support.

[IMAGE 6]

The result is consistent staff answers. A customer asking about a return policy hears the same answer from the newest boy and the most senior staff member, because they both learned it from the same voice. Rao garu told me he hears "your staff said something else" far less often now. I won't put a figure in his mouth, but he was clearly happy about it.

## Why consistent staff answers matter so much when the owner is away

When an owner is in the store, mistakes get corrected on the spot. A staff member gives a customer the wrong price, the owner hears it and fixes it. A staff member tells a customer the wrong return rule, the owner steps in. But a shop owner on leave can't step in, and small wrong answers pile up into lost customers.

That is why we spent so much time on consistent staff answers before Rao garu took his first holiday. We collected every question customers ask often: return rules, home delivery limits, offer conditions, product locations, payment options. For each one, Rao garu recorded his answer once, and that answer went into the store's permanent memory and the training audio.

Now, when any staff member is unsure, he asks the store's assistant through a small earpiece, and the answer comes in Rao garu's words. The newest boy and the oldest staff member give the same answer. Customers stop hearing "let me ask sir" and start hearing a clear reply.

I have seen the opposite in many shops on my travels. The owner leaves for two days, and when he returns, he finds that three customers were told three different things, and one of them has already posted an angry review. ## The first month for a shop owner on leave

Nobody should build this system and go on holiday the next day. The first month is for practice, with the owner still in the store, watching how the staff respond.

In Rao garu's first month, he stayed in the store but used only the app. Instead of walking to the billing counter, he tapped. Instead of calling a staff member, he sent a message through the sound box. Instead of giving the morning briefing himself, he let the recorded briefing play and stood at the back, listening.

This month showed us many small things to fix. Some speakers were too loud near the billing counter. Some pre-written lines sounded too formal for Rao garu, so he rewrote them. One staff member didn't respond to his name because the voice clone pronounced it slightly differently, so we corrected the pronunciation. By the end of the month, the staff were so used to the system that his physical presence made little difference.

Only then did he plan his first real leave. And when he came back, he told me the most important thing he gained was the confidence that he could leave and the store would still feel like his store.

## How a shop owner on leave can manage staff remotely: Rao garu's first full holiday

The real test came when Rao garu finally went to Andhra Pradesh for a family function, the first time in years that he stayed for the whole event. He sat with his relatives, ate properly, attended every ceremony, and checked the app a few times a day.

From his phone, he could manage staff remotely, as if he were standing in the store. He saw the morning briefing play on time. He watched the billing queue on the CCTV and opened a second counter with two taps. He praised the shelf team by name after a busy afternoon. He recorded a temporary instruction about a delayed supplier and set it to play once. And he saw the day's sales before going to bed.

His family told me later that the staff behaved better that week than in many weeks when Rao garu was present, because the owner's voice was everywhere and nobody knew when he was watching. Rao garu laughed when he heard this and said he would have to go on leave more often.

## The whole family runs it now

What makes me happiest about this project is that it is no longer one man's tool. The younger members of his family handle the technical side and update the system. Others use the app to manage the evening shift when he wants to rest. Other family members can step in when needed, each with their own access.

This is something I believe in deeply, and I wrote about it in [business ideas for women entrepreneurs and family ambition](https://upcomingtools.com/business-ideas-women-entrepreneurs-family-ambition/). A family business should not depend on one person's presence. When the knowledge, the voice and the controls are shared, the family can share the load, and the owner can finally be a father, a husband and a son again, as well as a shopkeeper.

And this ecosystem can be scaled for anyone. A small kirana shop might start with only the sound box and the morning briefing. A larger store might use everything Rao garu uses. The design is the same; only the size changes.

On this Diwali trip, I met many owners like the Rao garu I first met: tied to their shops, missing family functions, repeating instructions, answering the same staff questions all day. I sat with them, I joked, I listened.

I never spoke to their staff about any of this, and I never mentioned money. When an owner asked how, I asked him to call his son or daughter, or a well-wisher who understands technology. I explained the idea to them, left my card and moved on.

## How a shop owner on leave can start small

If you are a shop owner who wants a holiday that is actually a holiday, here is how I would begin.

Start with your voice and your daily routine. Write down the instructions you give every morning and record them in your own voice. Even if you do nothing else, a morning briefing that plays on its own will save you time every day.

Then add a simple sound box in the store and connect it to an app on your phone, so you can send instructions from anywhere. After that, connect your CCTV, add staff names and mood buttons, and finally build staff training videos.

Do it in steps. Let your staff get used to each part before adding the next. And involve your family from the beginning, because the system is strongest when more than one person can run it.

## Questions People Ask About a Shop Owner on Leave

**How can a shop owner on leave still manage the shop?**

With a remote shop management app connected to CCTV and a store sound box, the owner can watch the shop, send instructions and speak to staff in his own voice from anywhere.

**What is a remote shop management app?**

It is a single app on the owner's phone that brings together staff lists, CCTV, payments and instructions, so he can run the store with a few taps instead of being present.

**What is an AI sound box for shop use?**

It is a set of speakers in the store that plays announcements and instructions, often in the owner's cloned voice, sent from his app or scheduled in advance.

**Can a staff announcement system call staff by name?**

Yes, the owner can tap one or more staff names and choose a pre-written message, so the sound box speaks to those people directly, such as sending four staff members for lunch.

**What are mood buttons in a shop app?**

They are groups of pre-written messages sorted by tone, such as polite, firm, appreciative or urgent, each with about ten options the owner can play or edit anytime.

**How does CCTV with speaker for shop areas work?**

Each camera zone is paired with a speaker, so when the owner sees something on the CCTV he can speak to that area immediately through the app.

**Is it fair to staff to use CCTV and speakers?**

It is fair when cameras cover only work areas, staff are told clearly how the system works, and the speakers are used for instructions and appreciation, never for public shouting.

**How do staff training videos help a store?**

Training videos in the owner's voice and the staff's languages teach everyone the same way of working, which leads to consistent staff answers for customers.

**Can instructions be scheduled to repeat?**

Yes, an owner can record an instruction, mark it permanent or temporary, and set it to play once, daily, weekly, hourly or every fifteen minutes.

**Can a small kirana shop use this system?**

Yes, a small shop can start with only a sound box and a recorded morning briefing, then add CCTV, staff buttons and training videos as it grows.
