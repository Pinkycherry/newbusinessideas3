Poster Composition Library

Reverse-engineered from the owner's 30 Pinterest poster references (9 Nov 2026). For every site. One composition per image, never repeated inside an article. No credits, cast, dates, director, producer or platform text.

  1. Symbol silhouette collage: a giant symbolic object (helmet, ledger, padlock) whose silhouette holds small story faces; monochrome; title top centre.
  2. Duotone graphic pose: one colour field, halftone dots, hero sprawled on a key prop, flowing script title.
  3. Diagonal torn-paper vignettes: three story moments stacked in diagonal paper strips, script title top.
  4. Giant face arc over life montage: huge face or gear sweeping across the top, the hero's world cascading below.
  5. Vintage postcard or stamp: aged paper, stamp marks, one strong colour accent object, rain or street scene.
  6. Puppeteer: a giant figure above controlling small figures with strings; dramatic sky.
  7. One-point perspective interior: hero centred in a symmetrical bus, shop or corridor, folk-art details, chunky distressed title.
  8. Stacked levels: a watcher peeking over a wall at the top, a group below hiding something.
  9. Vehicle side profile: auto, tempo or scooter in side view, pastel sky, slice of life, handwritten title.
  10. Two faces above, lone figure below: emotional faces large in the sky, a small figure walking into smoke.
  11. Night rooftop minimal: two figures, cool palette, clean small title at top.
  12. Retro grain split: textured vintage poster, two people separated by a central object.
  13. Vertical strip panels: faces in red-tinted vertical strips, a central figure holding the key object.
  14. Mirrored profiles: two profiles facing each other framing a central face, city horizon and tape below.
  15. Colour isolation in a crowd: hero in colour among a uniform, veiled or grey crowd.
  16. Turning back in a dark crowd: lone figure looking over the shoulder in a sea of dark suits.
  17. Back-to-back rivals on torn paper texture.
  18. Elder in foreground, distant figure in landscape, pylons or fields.
  19. Low-angle roar: mid-shout, fire, chains, dust, heroic scale.
  20. Tiny figure against giant threat: a wave, a wall of paper or a crashing price board between buildings.
  21. Vertigo tower: extreme high angle down a tall structure, figure clinging at top.
  22. Falling between walls: symmetrical void, figure in free fall.
  23. Face pyramid: stacked faces rising to one figure at the top, small town at the base.
  24. Shattered glass: a face split by cracked glass, other faces in the shards.
  25. Split horizon: warm portrait above, cold threat below with a measuring scale or meter.
  26. Ghost repeats: central portrait with faded repeats of the hero in windows or mirrors.
  27. Mid-leap action: hero leaping with a prop against forest or market, dust.
  28. Emotion over action: close embrace or handshake above, a charge or chase below in mist.
  29. Rain candid two-shot: wet street, hand-lettered script title, tagline underneath.
  30. Monument backdrop: landmark, birds, pastel sky, relaxed pair in foreground.
  31. Train-window double exposure: the hero's face reflected large in a moving train window, the passing fields, platforms or river running inside the reflection, a second person seated beyond the glass.
  32. Ghat-steps timeline: long diagonal stone steps descending to a river, the hero striding down while story figures stand on lower steps at intervals like moments in a timeline.
  33. Ledger landscape: the whole scene drawn inside the ruled lines of a giant open account ledger, handwritten figures forming the horizon, the hero standing on the page.
  34. Lamp-light circle: one diya-lit circle in deep darkness holding the hero and one other person, the rest of the story shown only as silhouettes around the edge.

Universal rule (9 Nov 2026)

Never write a composition number in a prompt. The image model does not know this library. Every prompt spells out the full layout in words: frame split, where the author stands and what he is doing, every other person, objects, place, light, palette, poster title and tagline, camera. Only the author's photo is supplied, so everything else must come from the prompt. Add new compositions (35, 36 and on) from each new set of reference posters.

Prompt order

concept line, composition number, hero placement and scale (reference face), story layer, location, light and palette, title text with typography and placement, tagline, texture and finish, camera only when photographic, --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 7, negatives (no credits, no dates, no logos, no random side characters).
