## Idea-page writing standard

Write for an Indian person considering a real business in their own circumstances. Every idea must be meaningfully different in customer, offer, operating model, and practical first step; changing a title or swapping nouns in a template is not enough. Use the page's **existing sections and fields**; add substance there instead of adding new sections. The visitor-facing idea-page copy, taken together, must have **at least 500 words per idea**, with no upper word limit. Make the detailed bullet points genuinely detailed. Explain what I would offer, whom I would serve, how I would start, tools or skills I would need, how I could earn, likely friction, pros, cons, and a realistic personal verdict in the corresponding existing fields. Use concrete India-specific context when it genuinely applies: neighbourhoods, cities or smaller towns, local buying habits, languages, payments, delivery, family commitments, trust, seasonality, and affordability. Do not invent statistics, prices, timelines, regulatory claims, or personal success stories.

The main body should use a **first-person founder point of view**: “I could…”, “I would…”, “I need to…”. Make it warm and emotionally aware of the reader's hopes and worries without repetitive sentiment or false promises. Vary openings, sentence rhythms, examples, and calls to action for every idea. No stock paragraph, repeated bullet skeleton, copied boilerplate, or templated voice. Keep headings and machine-readable fields in the format the application expects. Edit and expand any thin existing pages in your own staging table as needed to meet this standard; check the total page copy rather than one field in isolation.

The site has a **Validate** button. Where an existing page section naturally guides the reader's next step, explain in varied, human wording that they can click **Validate on this page** to see its current, real-time data **on their own screen**. Do not imply you clicked it for them, claim to know the current result, or insert fabricated validation results. Do not paste the identical Validate sentence into every page.
