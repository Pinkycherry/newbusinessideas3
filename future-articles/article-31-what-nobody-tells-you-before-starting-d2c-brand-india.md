# Starting a D2C brand in India: costs founders discover too late

I took my coffee onto the balcony on a weekend and kept thinking about a product page I had seen the previous day. The photographs were careful, the name sounded expensive, and every button made buying feel easy. I could imagine the work behind the page. I could also imagine the parcel going to the wrong address, the customer refusing delivery, and the founder trying to work out whether that first order made money. The page could tell me the price. It could not tell me what remained after the order was finished.

Starting a D2C brand in India can begin with an excellent product and still stumble on ordinary arithmetic. The product has to be made, packed, found, bought, shipped, sometimes returned, and often explained by a person after delivery. Each step takes cash or time. A pretty margin on the product itself tells only a fraction of the story.

I would be glad to see more founders build small brands with character. I would be more glad to see them survive their first demanding season. Before you register a name, decide which order economics you will record and which customer problem your product solves well enough for people to come back. The name can wait while you test that.

## The gap between the pitch deck version and the real version

The simple pitch goes like this: make a product, place it on your own site, attract customers and keep the margin that a retailer would have taken. It leaves out the retailer's work of bringing people to a shelf, holding stock, explaining the product, taking returns and collecting payment. Direct selling moves those jobs onto your own calendar and ledger. You may gain control over presentation and the customer relationship. You also own the interruptions.

I would write the journey of one order from the customer's first encounter to the final payment settlement. Who made the product? Where was it stored? Which advertisement, search result or recommendation brought the buyer? What did the checkout promise? Who packed and shipped it? When was the money available to pay the supplier? Who answered the buyer's complaint? If a founder cannot trace these steps for a small batch, a larger launch will hide the same problem under a larger stack of parcels.

D2C business ideas in India are sometimes pitched as if an online store removes geography. The website can be opened from anywhere; delivery still has a starting point, a destination, serviceable pin codes and an actual carrier. Packaging that protects glass across one city may fail on a longer route. A cold-chain product cannot be treated like a printed notebook. Test the real shipment before selling a national promise.

Consider a hypothetical founder selling a concentrated cleaning product. A customer may like the refill idea but worry that it will leak. The product has to work; the bottle, closure, instructions and outer parcel have to survive handling. A lower packaging expense in the founder's spreadsheet can create a much higher support and replacement burden. That is one of the direct to consumer brand challenges a product photograph cannot settle.

The product itself has a legal life too. A packaged commodity can require prescribed declarations, and the requirements depend on what is sold. The Department of Consumer Affairs explains [legal metrology declarations for packaged goods](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2088051&lang=2&reg=48), including registration matters for manufacturers, packers and importers. A founder should check the current requirements for the actual product with a qualified person before printing a large batch of labels. Food and cosmetics bring other product-specific obligations. Compliance is part of the first batch, not a decoration for a successful one.

Ecommerce business ideas with low investment often mean a small first inventory, a simple website and manual fulfilment. They do not mean that customers will arrive without effort or that mistakes are free. Small batches can cost more per unit and make quality variation easier to notice. They are still useful because they expose the real operation while there is room to change it. I would pay for a small honest test before paying for a warehouse full of confidence.

### The promise the site makes on your behalf

Read the product page as a buyer who has never met you. Can they tell size, material, use, delivery conditions and what happens if something is wrong? If the photograph makes a product look twice as large as it is, you will spend the next month explaining scale. If the colour shifts under studio lights, say so and show it in ordinary light. The goal is a sale the customer can live with after opening the parcel.

The government's [explanation of consumer protections for e-commerce](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1639925) are a reminder that online sellers need accurate, accessible information about matters such as returns, refunds and delivery. Have a qualified adviser check how the rules apply to your selling model. A policy written only to reduce refunds may generate disputes and damage trust. Write what you can perform and build the operation to meet it.

## What starting a D2C brand in India does to the margin

Start with the unit you sell. A selling price includes product cost, packaging, payment processing, the fulfilment work, outward shipping, expected support, marketing and the share of returns or damage. Taxes and credits need their own correct treatment; ask an accountant to keep them from being casually added to or subtracted from profit. Some costs occur per parcel, others per month. A founder can mistake a positive amount left after manufacturing and shipping for profit while rent, photography and customer acquisition sit in separate tabs.

I would separate three numbers without inventing a universal percentage. First, the contribution from a completed order before shared overhead. Second, the cash tied up before that order is settled. Third, the founder's actual monthly costs, including people and their own time where the business must eventually pay for that work. A strong contribution does not prevent a cash shortage when inventory is paid for early and collections arrive later. A full warehouse is an asset on paper and an unpaid bill in the founder's week.

The table I would put beside a product page is less glamorous than a brand board. It is also more useful after a bad month.

| Cost category | What a new founder may underestimate | What to record in a small test |
|---|---|---|
| Product and quality control | Rework, imperfect units and samples used in testing | Units made, units sellable and the cause of rejects |
| Packaging | Protective material, printed labels and manual packing time | Damage by pack type and time spent on each parcel |
| Payment and settlement | Processing charges, failed payments and timing of available cash | Gross checkout amount, deductions and settlement date |
| Shipping and failed delivery | Pin-code differences, surcharges and return-to-origin parcels | Quoted cost beside charged cost and delivery outcome |
| Customer acquisition | Creative work, discounts and spend on visitors who do not buy | Spend by channel and orders attributable with reasonable confidence |
| Returns and support | Reverse movement, inspection, replacement and refunds | Reason, condition on arrival and final disposition |
| Ongoing operations | Storage, software, staff and founder time | Monthly outlay and who performed each recurring task |

D2C brand profitability reality appears only when you follow an order to its end. If a parcel returns, the first outward freight may be gone, reverse transport may be due, and the item may need cleaning, repacking or disposal. A refund also changes how the original marketing expense should be judged. The useful unit is a completed customer relationship, with the money and work it required, rather than the number of checkout confirmations.

D2C startup mistakes to avoid include setting a retail price by adding an appealing percentage to production cost. A founder needs to know what the total delivered order costs and what happens at different order sizes. A single lightweight item and a two-item basket can have different economics. Do not assume a larger basket always saves you; heavier or fragile parcels can change the carrier charge. Test the exact combinations customers buy.

For a food brand, manufacturing and distribution rules can add still more cost and responsibility. The [official FoSCoS portal](https://foscos.fssai.gov.in/) is the route to check food-business registration or licensing and related processes. Whether you make, pack, store or sell the food matters. Ask a qualified food-compliance professional about the actual product and activity. A generic D2C checklist should never replace product-specific safety work.

### Use a margin worksheet that can be contradicted

I would make one row per test order, recording the product, price paid after discount, channel, packaging, shipping charge, payment charge, delivery outcome and support time. Put estimated costs beside actual invoices. When a surprise appears, leave it visible. A worksheet that always confirms the launch plan is a sales presentation, not a management tool.

For shared expenses, choose a simple and consistent allocation, then show the result both with and without it. You might have paid once for photographs that support several months of orders. Spreading that cost across a fantasy number of future orders hides the immediate cash outlay. Show the cash paid now and a reasonable allocation for comparing products. Do not confuse the two views.

I would also inspect discounts. A launch coupon can be a useful experiment if it tells you whether the right buyer understands the product. A permanent discount needed to make every first sale may be a pricing or value problem. If the buyer never sees the regular price except beside a crossed-out number, treat the discounted amount as the real selling price in your model.

## The d2c brand customer acquisition cost founders underplan

An order can arrive through a friend, a search result, an advertisement, a creator or a repeat buyer. These routes cost different things, some of which do not appear as an ad-platform charge. A founder's evenings answering messages, a free sample sent to a creator, and a discount in a referral code all belong in the assessment. You do not need a perfect attribution system to know that a customer was expensive to find. You do need to stop calling every organic order free.

Building a d2c brand from scratch means earning enough trust for someone to buy from a site they have not used. That work includes clear product evidence, reliable replies, secure checkout, honest delivery estimates and reviews that reflect actual customers. Advertising can put the product in front of people. It cannot create an explanation where the page offers only adjectives.

The d2c brand customer acquisition cost should be examined by a cohort of customers over time, with care about what you can truly observe. Record which buyers return, when, for what product and without which discounts. If a customer buys only once because the item lasts a long time, build the first order to stand on its own. Do not plug a repeat rate into the model because another brand sells a consumable product. The frequency of use belongs to your product and your buyer.

I would run a small channel test with a defined question. For a notebook, perhaps the question is whether working adults respond to a demonstration of its paper under ordinary pens. For a refill product, perhaps it is whether customers understand the dilution instructions without a staff member explaining them. Change one part of the message and compare the questions people ask. A channel test that ends with "the views were high" has not measured whether an order can be earned at a sustainable cost.

D2C business ideas for beginners can start with an audience the founder already understands well, but familiarity is not proof of demand. Ask prospective buyers to compare a prototype with what they currently use. Watch whether they pay, return, complain and tell another person in their own words what changed. A poll with enthusiastic clicks cannot settle quality, delivery and repeat purchase.

The [ONDC network's seller participant page](https://www.ondc.org/pages/seller-network-participants.html) describes participants that connect sellers, digitise catalogues and handle related network functions. It can be one route to discovery and commerce, with its own participants and operating terms. It does not make acquisition cost vanish. Compare the fees, control, support and settlement of each channel using the actual agreement available to your business.

### The customer question hiding in the support inbox

Before buying more traffic, read every support request from the test batch. Are customers asking what the product does, how to use it or where their parcel is? The first two may require better product information or design. The third may require a realistic delivery promise and better tracking. Answering the question once in a product page or instruction card can save repeated messages, but only if you check whether the next customers understand it.

I would write down the exact customer phrase rather than translate it into marketing language. "I thought it would fit in my bag" says more about size expectations than "consumer perception gap." "I could not open the cap" says more than "packaging friction." Those sentences tell a founder what to show in photographs and what to test at the packing table.

## Logistics and returns, the unglamorous half of the business

D2C logistics challenges in India vary by product, route and carrier. A parcel needs the correct address, a carrier able to serve that location, packing that survives its journey and a process when delivery fails. Speak to actual carriers about serviceable pin codes, pickup schedules, claims, weight measurement, COD if offered and return handling. Read the commercial agreement. A checkout promise made before checking those facts becomes a customer service problem after the sale.

The [India Post Parcel contractual service page](https://www.indiapost.gov.in/mailproducts/premiumservices/businessparcel) describes a parcel service for contractual customers, including a cash-on-delivery option. It is one possible logistics route, with current terms to check for your product and area. The relevant question is not which carrier sounds biggest. It is which carrier can deliver the parcels you sell, confirm outcomes and handle exceptions at a cost you understand.

Cash on delivery can remove one hesitation at checkout while creating another kind of risk. A buyer who places an order can be unavailable or refuse the parcel. A founder may pay for outward movement and a return without ever making a completed sale. Verify addresses and communicate the delivery expectation respectfully. Do not use aggressive calls or misleading urgency to force a confirmation. Track COD and prepaid outcomes separately so one apparent conversion rate does not hide very different completed-order economics.

Returns need a route before the first order. Decide how the customer contacts you, how eligibility is checked under applicable law and policy, who books pickup, how the item is inspected, when the refund is made and what happens to the returned unit. A slow, confusing refund can cost more in trust than the item itself. An overly casual process can also invite repeated preventable losses. Write a fair policy and train whoever answers the customer.

One of the direct to consumer brand challenges I would take seriously is the time between a return request and knowing whether the product can be sold again. A garment may arrive used or damaged. A personal-care item may be unsafe to resell after opening. A home object may need repacking. The category changes the recovery options; no blanket "return rate" assumption fits them all. Keep inspection notes and reasons rather than a single column labelled returned.

The d2c logistics challenges in India become obvious when a founder tests parcels outside their comfortable local circle. Send prototypes through the routes the business intends to serve. Ask testers for photographs of the parcel on arrival and their first impression of the item. Record the weight charged by the carrier beside the weight you estimated. These are not market statistics; they are measurements the founder can make for their own product.

### A failed delivery is a product lesson too

Sometimes the address is incomplete. Sometimes a delivery promise was too ambitious. Sometimes the buyer misunderstood the product and changed their mind before arrival. Record the reason from more than one source when possible: carrier status, customer message and your own timeline. Treat each failed delivery as a clue to a specific fix. A blanket campaign to "reduce returns" cannot tell you which part of the journey is failing.

If the product breaks in transit, review packaging with the supplier and carrier. If the buyer refuses it after seeing the size, improve the listing. If the parcel repeatedly reaches an unserviceable address, change the checkout validation and delivery promise. Different causes require different work. A founder who measures only the final percentage can spend money on the wrong solution.

## What a realistic first year of starting a D2C brand in India looks like

I would plan the first year as a sequence of decisions rather than a growth curve drawn before the product is shipped. Begin with a small, compliant batch and a narrow buyer. Verify that the product can be made consistently and delivered intact. Test whether people understand it without your explanation. Record complete orders, returns and cash timing. Decide whether a second batch deserves more investment. That is already a demanding operating year.

Ecommerce business ideas with low investment are well suited to this discipline when the first batch can be modest. Handmade or locally produced items can reveal demand without a huge stock commitment, but quality and availability still have to be reliable. A small test should include strangers who can tell you no. If every buyer is a friend helping the founder, ask what the next buyer will see when the favour disappears.

D2C startup mistakes to avoid include ordering an impressive range before discovering which one product people understand. More variants create more stock decisions, more photographs, more labels and more opportunities for a picker to send the wrong item. A focused test can still offer meaningful choice, but each choice should answer a buyer's need rather than fill the website grid.

The d2c brand profitability reality may stay uncomfortable for a while. A founder should know whether a repeatable order can contribute after its direct costs and what volume would support the ongoing operation, without assuming that volume will arrive. The test may show that the price must change, the pack must change, or the channel is wrong. It may show that the product is loved but too costly to ship individually. That finding can suggest a bundle or local distribution experiment. Do not describe every adjustment as a pivot; often it is ordinary product work.

I would make a weekly cash check while orders are still few. List the supplier invoices due, inventory already paid for, carrier bills, refunds promised and payment settlements expected. An order can be profitable under a sensible allocation and still leave too little money in the account to pay next week's packaging invoice. If the schedule does not work, reduce the next batch, renegotiate an honest payment timetable or change the selling method. Do not treat a customer's pending refund as cash you can spend. The ledger and the bank balance should tell a coherent story before you add more advertising.

I would reserve time to learn what buyers do after delivery. Do they use the item correctly? Do they buy again when it runs out? Do they give a reason when they return it? Ask permission before using their words in promotion. A complaint that changes the product can be more valuable than a vague compliment. Pay attention to the quiet customer who never complains but never returns.

D2C business ideas for beginners are easier to test when the founder can describe a single customer, use occasion and reason to believe the product. That description should be revisable. If actual buyers differ from the imagined audience, investigate why. Perhaps the pack size fits a workplace better than a household. Perhaps the problem is more urgent in one climate or season. Record what people do before remaking the brand around a guess.

The first year also includes administrative work. Check the relevant GST treatment, invoices, product permissions, consumer disclosures and data handling with qualified advisers. A food seller should confirm food requirements; a cosmetics seller needs its own applicable checks; a seller of packaged goods should verify declarations. Do the product-specific work before scaling an advertisement. An order gained through a misleading or noncompliant listing is no victory.

### Decide what evidence earns the next batch

Set a few conditions before spending the next tranche of money. The product reaches customers in usable condition. You can explain the cost of a completed order. Buyers can describe what they bought and how to use it. Returns have named causes. Cash from sales arrives in time to pay the obligations you have taken on, or you have a sensible reserve. These are operational tests, not universal benchmarks. Their values will differ by product.

If the conditions fail, do the narrowest useful repair. A leaking cap may need a design change, not a new agency. A confusing size chart may need better measurements, not another discount. A high delivery failure in certain locations may require a different carrier or a restricted promise. A founder who can name the fault has more options than one who sees only a disappointing revenue total.

When the conditions hold, expansion can be deliberate. Increase a batch, add a channel or test another region while keeping the original ledger visible. If a new channel produces more orders but fewer completed, profitable ones, investigate before declaring a breakthrough. Growth that uses more cash each week than the founder can replace is a real constraint, however attractive the order counter looks.

## Frequently asked questions before launching a D2C brand

### Can a D2C brand start with a small amount of inventory?
Often, if the product and supplier allow a small compliant test batch. Include sample, packaging, shipping, marketing and return costs in the test. A small batch is useful because it exposes product and fulfilment faults while they are still manageable; it is not proof that customers will be cheap to acquire.

### Should I offer cash on delivery from the first day?
Decide after checking carrier terms and your ability to absorb failed delivery and return-to-origin costs. COD may suit some buyers, but completed orders and collections matter more than checkout confirmations. Track COD separately from prepaid orders and communicate delivery clearly.

### How do I calculate customer acquisition cost for a new brand?
Record channel spending, creative work, samples, discounts and the completed customers reasonably linked to each activity. Keep uncertainty visible where attribution is weak. Compare that cost with the contribution from actual completed orders and realistic repeat behaviour for your product.

### What should the return policy say?
It should clearly explain eligibility, the request route, pickup or shipping process, refund timing and any applicable product-specific limits, consistently with consumer law. Make sure staff can perform the promise. Have a qualified person check the wording for your category and selling model.

### Is a good gross product margin enough to justify launch?
No. Include packaging, payment, marketing, fulfilment, support, returns and the monthly operation. Check when cash leaves and returns as well as what an order contributes. Test with real parcels and real invoices before scaling the batch.

## A closing word before you register the brand name

That product page from my balcony thought is worth building, but the honest version of the story begins after a customer pays. Starting a D2C brand in India calls for a product someone wants and an operation that can keep the promise on the page. D2C business ideas in India deserve the same scrutiny you would give a physical shop's stockroom and cash drawer.

Building a d2c brand from scratch can be satisfying work if the first tests let you change your mind. A year from now, a founder who has measured completed orders, fixed the packaging and listened to returns will have more than a polished name: a product and operating method they can responsibly repeat.
