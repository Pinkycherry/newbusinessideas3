# Article writing rules — the 50-article batch

Read this and `03-TONE-AND-PERSONA.md` before writing any article from
`01-RESEARCH-TABLE-50-ARTICLES.md`. This batch is portable — written to run
on bbusiness.online, businessidea.io, a future dedicated domain, or
anywhere else, so nothing here assumes a specific site's database or
components. That is the one hard difference from `BLOG_CONTENT_STANDARDS.md`:
these articles don't use this site's `ValidateButton`/`CalculatorWidget`
embeds, because they may never run on this site at all.

## Why this batch exists: Google AdSense approval, not a traffic number

Added 2026-10-04, the founder's own words: the actual goal of this batch
is getting approved for Google AdSense. Not because traffic from search is
the plan — Google's AI Mode is already pulling the large majority of
search traffic into its own answers instead of sending clicks to the
source site, and that's expected to keep happening, not reverse. AdSense
approval matters anyway, because it's an outside, independently-earned
signal: Google itself looking at this content and certifying it as real,
high-quality, trustworthy writing. That certification is worth more than
a traffic number — it's proof of conduct, the kind of third-party trust
mark a reader (or another business) can check for themselves.

Every one of the 50 articles has to be able to stand on its own in front
of that review. Concretely, that means **E-E-A-T** isn't a theory to nod
at, it's a checklist every article gets held to:

- **Experience.** The narrator has actually sat with these exact kinds of
  businesses — reads as lived, specific, a little messy, not a composed
  summary of what a business consultant would say. The intro devices in
  `03-TONE-AND-PERSONA.md` exist specifically to carry this.
- **Expertise.** The mechanics are *correct* — GST, Udyam, MSME lending,
  whatever the article touches, explained the way someone who's actually
  dealt with it explains it, not the way a search-result summary explains
  it.
- **Authoritativeness.** A confident, consistent, opinionated voice.
  Never the hedging, on-the-fence, "it depends on many factors" tone a
  generic AI assistant defaults to — this narrator has a position and says
  it.
- **Trustworthiness.** Zero fabricated numbers about any business, cost,
  or market (unchanged from every other rule in this project). Real,
  fetched-and-confirmed external links. Honest "this won't work for
  everyone" caveats where they're true, not everywhere as a hedge.

A Google reviewer — human or algorithmic — needs to be able to open any
single one of the 50 and recognize deep, consistent, non-templated
subject expertise paired with genuinely skilled writing. That is the real
bar for this batch, not a nice-to-have on top of it.

## Length

**3,000–6,000 words.** Each article in the research table gets its own
word-count *band* (e.g. 3,000–4,200; 4,100–5,300), never a single fixed
number, and the bands themselves don't step down the list in a visible
sequence — picked per-topic, same discipline as every other batch here.
Write to what the topic actually needs inside its band; don't pad to hit
a ceiling, don't stop short of the floor.

## Keywords — ten per article, placed naturally

- **One focus keyword.** Appears in 2–3 headings, with natural grammar —
  never force a heading into an awkward shape just to fit the exact
  phrase.
- **Nine additional keywords.** Each used naturally 2–3 times across the
  body and occasionally in a heading. Never a fragment stuffed into a
  sentence that wouldn't otherwise exist.
- **Non-saturated, researched, not generic.** "Business ideas" repeated
  with no qualifier is the thing to avoid — every keyword should combine
  the business angle with something specific: the audience, the capital
  tier, the region, the mechanism. Research each one before locking it in,
  the same way `BLOG_BATCH_2_TOPICS.md`'s keywords were cross-checked
  against what a brand-new domain can realistically rank for.

## SEO title and slug

- **SEO title: 60–80 characters.** Contains the focus keyword, the target
  audience, and a business-specific word — not a bare generic phrase.
- **Slug: under 75 characters.** Contains the focus keyword, the audience,
  and a business-related word, same as the title's logic, shortened.
- **H1 matches the SEO title's intent and contains the focus keyword.**
  This was missed on earlier blog posts — the live H1s didn't carry the
  focus keyword or any additional keyword at all. Every article in this
  batch checks that its own H1 actually contains its own focus keyword
  before being considered done.

## Structure — dynamic, never templated

Every article's heading structure (H1, every H2, every H3, every table
heading) is planned out in full in its own row of the research table
*before* the article gets written — not decided on the fly, not reused
from the article before it. No two articles in this batch share the same
shape: if one is a numbered comparison, the next is a narrative essay with
a single mid-article table, the one after that is a myth-vs-reality piece
with no table at all. The research table's own "Pattern" column exists to
make this checkable — read the last 3–4 rows' patterns before deciding the
next one.

## Linking

- **No internal links required.** This batch doesn't assume a destination
  site yet, so there's nothing fixed to link to internally. If a specific
  destination is confirmed later, internal links get added then, not now.
- **5–10 external links per article, every one real and verified** —
  fetch and confirm each one actually resolves before using it, the same
  standard `CONTENT_ENGINE_PLAN.md` already set for idea pages. The
  authoritative pool for this niche: `msme.gov.in`, `startupindia.gov.in`,
  `udyamregistration.gov.in`, `sidbi.in`, `nsic.co.in`, plus RBI, GST, and
  Income Tax portals where a specific claim calls for one, and recognized
  business-research sources (not forums, not AI-aggregator sites) where a
  government source doesn't cover the specific point.
- **Links go inline, as anchor text inside a real sentence.** Never a
  "Sources" list, never a bullet-point dump at the end of the article.
  This is the single most strictly prohibited formatting mistake for this
  batch — if a draft has an end-of-article link list, that draft is wrong,
  full stop.

## Interactive and visual elements

- **Tables and simple structured elements only for this batch.** No
  complex infographics, no custom dashboards, no generated chart images —
  those don't port cleanly into a WordPress block editor or a generic site
  the way a plain HTML table does, and attempting them here risks the kind
  of damage a half-working graphic does to a page. A plain, well-labeled
  table carries a cost comparison or a timeline exactly as well.
- Each article's table(s) are planned in the research table alongside its
  headings — how many, and what each one actually compares.
- If a specific article later turns out to genuinely need a chart or
  diagram, that's a case-by-case call made at drafting time, flagged
  loudly, not something to default into.

## The intro/outro and the uniqueness log

Every article picks one intro device and one outro device from
`03-TONE-AND-PERSONA.md`'s banks — never the same device as the article
immediately before it, and varied in actual wording every time even when
the same device number repeats later in the batch. Record the choice in
the log table at the bottom of `01-RESEARCH-TABLE-50-ARTICLES.md` as each
article gets written, and read that log before starting the next one.

## Before calling any article done, check:

**Do check #2 as a literal count, not an impression.** Article 1 in this
batch was written with confident, well-structured prose and genuinely
zero occurrences of any of its 9 additional keywords — only the focus
keyword showed up, and even that wasn't confirmed in headings. The
article read fine. It failed the one thing the whole keyword-research
step existed for, because the check was skipped under the momentum of
writing good sentences. Before saving any article, take the exact
keyword list from its row, and for each of the 10 phrases, actually
search the draft text (case-insensitive) and count the hits. Not a
feeling that it's probably in there — a count.

1. Word count inside its assigned band.
2. Focus keyword in the H1 and in 2–3 headings; all nine additional
   keywords present, naturally, 2–3 times each — counted, per the note
   above, before saving.
3. SEO title 60–80 characters; slug under 75; both contain the focus
   keyword.
4. 5–10 external links, inline, every one fetched and confirmed live.
5. No end-of-article link list.
6. Structure matches its own planned row, and differs from the last few
   articles' patterns.
7. Intro and outro devices don't repeat the previous article's; both
   logged.
8. Zero fabricated numbers about any business, cost, or market. Zero AI
   vendor named. Zero emojis. Voice DNA grammar rules followed.
9. E-E-A-T check: a real, specific lived moment (Experience); the
   mechanics are actually correct (Expertise); the voice is confident and
   opinionated, not hedging (Authoritativeness); every number and link
   traces to something real (Trustworthiness). This is the AdSense bar —
   check it on every article, not just the first few.

## The quality-check agent — briefing for the agent that reviews each finished article

Every article gets reviewed by a separate agent immediately after it's
written, before moving to the next one. That agent reads all three
documents in this folder first (`01-RESEARCH-TABLE-50-ARTICLES.md`, this
file, `03-TONE-AND-PERSONA.md`), then reads the finished article, and
checks for exactly these failure modes — report each one found, don't
just give a pass/fail:

1. **AI slop and AI tone.** Negative-parallelism ("Not X. Y." / "X isn't
   about Y, it's about Z."), em dashes, the banned-vocabulary list (delve,
   leverage, effortless, seamless, robust, crucial, holistic, and the
   rest), meta-commentary about the writing itself, "it's important to
   note," a "nobody" sentence opener, title-case headers. Any of these is
   a finding.
2. **Missing authoritativeness.** Hedging language, on-the-fence framing,
   a voice that sounds like a summary of what someone might think instead
   of a specific person's actual opinion.
3. **A personal number that shouldn't be there.** Any digit attached to
   the narrator's age, years of experience (in either the business/MSME
   track or the writing track), or a specific start date/year — these
   stay unnumbered until the founder sets the real anchor date. Flag any
   that slipped in.
4. **An imaginary number about the market.** Any statistic, cost, income
   figure, or market claim that isn't a real, checkable fact — flag it
   even if it sounds plausible, especially if it sounds plausible.
5. **Broken or missing external links.** Every external link actually
   fetched and confirmed to resolve, not assumed. Fewer than 5 or more
   than 10 external links is also a finding. Any link sitting in an
   end-of-article list instead of inline in a sentence is a finding on
   its own, regardless of whether the link itself is valid.
6. **Continuity and consistency breaks.** The intro/outro devices used
   don't match what the article's own row in the research table assigned,
   or repeat the immediately preceding article's devices. The structure
   doesn't match its planned row. The word count falls outside its
   assigned band.
7. **Tone drift.** Anything that reads like a different person wrote it
   than the persona in `03-TONE-AND-PERSONA.md` describes — too formal,
   too generic, too much like a template, not enough like the specific
   MBA-mentor narrator.

The agent reports findings back, corrections get applied, and only then
does the uniqueness log in `01-RESEARCH-TABLE-50-ARTICLES.md` get updated
and the next article starts.
